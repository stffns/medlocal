# WHO: Anaphylaxis and Severe Allergic Reactions

**When to Suspect Anaphylaxis.**
Rapid onset (minutes to hours) of TWO or more of:
- **Skin:** hives, itching, flushing, swelling of face/lips/tongue
- **Respiratory:** difficulty breathing, wheezing, stridor, throat tightness, hoarse voice
- **Cardiovascular:** dizziness, fainting, weak rapid pulse, low blood pressure
- **GI:** nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhea

OR: Known allergen exposure + respiratory compromise or hypotension alone

**Common Triggers.**
- Insect stings (bee, wasp, ant)
- Foods (peanuts, tree nuts, shellfish, eggs, milk)
- Medications (antibiotics especially penicillin, NSAIDs)
- Latex
- Unknown trigger in up to 20% of cases

**Immediate Treatment — EPINEPHRINE FIRST.**
**Step 1: Epinephrine (Adrenaline) — GIVE IMMEDIATELY:**
- **Adults and children >30 kg:** Epinephrine 0.5 mg IM (0.5 mL of 1:1000)
- **Children 15-30 kg:** Epinephrine 0.3 mg IM (0.3 mL of 1:1000)
- **Children <15 kg:** Epinephrine 0.15 mg IM (0.15 mL of 1:1000)
- **Inject into mid-outer thigh** (through clothing if needed)
- **Repeat every 5-15 minutes** if no improvement (up to 3 doses)
- Do NOT delay epinephrine — it is the only life-saving treatment

**Step 2: Position the Patient:**
- If breathing difficulty: sit upright
- If dizzy/faint/low BP: lie flat with legs elevated
- If unconscious: recovery position (on side)
- If pregnant: lie on left side
- Do NOT stand the patient up suddenly (risk of cardiac arrest)

**Step 3: Call for Help / Prepare for Transport:**
- **REFER IMMEDIATELY** to nearest health facility
- Monitor airway, breathing, and circulation continuously
- Be prepared to give CPR

**Step 4: Additional Treatments (after epinephrine):**
1. **Oxygen** if available (high flow, 10-15 L/min by face mask)
2. **IV fluids** if available and trained: normal saline 20 mL/kg bolus for hypotension
3. **Salbutamol inhaler** for persistent wheezing (4-8 puffs via spacer)
4. **Antihistamine** (chlorpheniramine or cetirizine) — helps itching/hives but does NOT treat anaphylaxis
5. **Hydrocortisone** 200 mg IV/IM — prevents biphasic reaction (delayed second episode)

**What NOT to Do.**
- Do NOT give antihistamines instead of epinephrine — they are too slow
- Do NOT delay epinephrine waiting for other medications
- Do NOT give epinephrine IV unless in cardiac arrest (IM only in the field)
- Do NOT apply tourniquet to limb with sting
- Do NOT try to remove insect stinger with tweezers (scrape it off with edge of card)

**Insect Sting Without Anaphylaxis.**
- Remove stinger by scraping (not squeezing)
- Wash with soap and water
- Apply cold compress for 10 minutes
- Paracetamol or ibuprofen for pain
- Oral antihistamine (cetirizine 10 mg) for itching/swelling
- Watch for 30 minutes for signs of anaphylaxis

**Severe Allergic Reaction (Not Anaphylaxis).**
- Localized swelling only (face, lips) WITHOUT breathing difficulty
- Give oral antihistamine (cetirizine 10 mg or chlorpheniramine 4 mg)
- Prednisolone 40 mg oral for significant swelling
- Observe for 4-6 hours — can progress to anaphylaxis
- **Refer if:** swelling involves tongue or throat, any breathing difficulty, dizziness

**After Anaphylaxis — Important.**
- Observe for minimum 6-12 hours (biphasic reaction can occur)
- Prescribe epinephrine auto-injector if available
- Refer to allergy specialist
- Educate: carry epinephrine at all times, wear medical alert bracelet
- Avoid known trigger

**Danger Signs — Refer Immediately.**
- Any difficulty breathing or noisy breathing after allergen exposure
- Swelling of tongue, throat, or lips
- Feeling faint or dizzy after sting/allergen exposure
- Previous anaphylaxis with new exposure to same allergen
- Not improving after 2 doses of epinephrine

Source: WHO Emergency Triage Assessment and Treatment (ETAT+); WHO Basic Emergency Care (2018); World Allergy Organization Anaphylaxis Guidelines (2020); Resuscitation Council UK Anaphylaxis Guidelines (2021)
