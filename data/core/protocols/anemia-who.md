# WHO: Anemia Management for Community Health Workers

## When to Suspect Anemia
- Pale palms, nail beds, conjunctiva (inner eyelids)
- Fatigue, weakness, dizziness
- Shortness of breath on exertion
- Fast heartbeat
- In children: poor growth, poor appetite

## Diagnosis
- Hemoglobin test (if available):
  - Children 6-59 months: <11 g/dL = anemia
  - Pregnant women: <11 g/dL = anemia
  - Non-pregnant women: <12 g/dL = anemia
  - Men: <13 g/dL = anemia
- Severe anemia: <7 g/dL — **REFER URGENTLY**
- If no lab: very pale palms = clinical anemia

## Treatment

### Iron Supplementation
**Children 6-59 months (treatment):**
- Iron syrup 3 mg/kg/day for 3 months
- Give with vitamin C (orange juice) to improve absorption
- Do NOT give with milk or tea (blocks absorption)

**Pregnant women (treatment):**
- Ferrous sulfate 120 mg elemental iron + folic acid 400 mcg daily
- Continue for 3 months after hemoglobin normalizes

**Prevention in pregnancy:**
- Iron 30-60 mg + folic acid 400 mcg daily throughout pregnancy

### Deworming (if in endemic area)
- Albendazole 400 mg single dose (or Mebendazole 500 mg)
- Safe in pregnancy after first trimester
- Repeat every 6 months

### Diet Counseling
- Iron-rich foods: dark leafy greens, beans, lentils, meat, liver, eggs
- Vitamin C foods with meals: citrus, tomatoes, peppers
- Avoid tea/coffee with meals (inhibits iron absorption)

## Danger Signs — Refer Immediately
- Severe pallor (very white palms)
- Difficulty breathing at rest
- Fast or irregular heartbeat
- Confusion or altered consciousness
- Severe anemia in pregnancy (risk of hemorrhage)
- Hemoglobin <7 g/dL — may need blood transfusion

Source: WHO Guideline on Iron Supplementation (2016); WHO Recommendations on Antenatal Care (2016)
