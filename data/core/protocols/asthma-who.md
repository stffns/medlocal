# WHO: Asthma Management for Community Health Workers

**⚠ Before Treating Wheezing as Asthma — Rule Out Foreign Body Aspiration.**
Wheezing in a child is NOT always asthma. Before giving salbutamol,
check for these red flags that point to a retained foreign body:

- **Sudden onset** while eating small food (peanuts, corn, seeds,
  grapes) or playing with small objects (beads, coins, batteries)
- **History of a choking episode** (coughing fit, gagging, turned
  blue) at the moment of onset
- **Unilateral wheeze** — wheezing heard on only ONE side of the chest
- **Unilateral decreased breath sounds** or asymmetric chest movement
- **No prior history** of asthma in the child
- **No fever, no runny nose**
- Peak age 6 months to 3 years

If ANY of these are present, see the Retained Foreign Body Aspiration
protocol. Salbutamol will not help a mechanical obstruction and delays
definitive care (rigid bronchoscopy). Refer urgently instead.

**When to Suspect Asthma.**
- Recurrent episodes of: wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness, cough
- Symptoms worse at night or early morning
- Triggered by: cold air, exercise, dust, smoke, animals, pollen
- Family history of asthma or allergies
- Symptoms improve with bronchodilator (salbutamol)

**Acute Asthma Attack — Assessment.**
**Mild/Moderate Attack:**
- Can talk in sentences
- Increased respiratory rate but not severe
- Wheezing
- SpO2 ≥90% (if pulse oximeter available)

**Severe Attack — URGENT:**
- Cannot complete sentences (speaks in words only)
- Respiratory rate >30/min (adult), >40/min (child)
- Using accessory muscles (neck muscles, intercostal retraction)
- SpO2 <90%
- Agitated

**Life-Threatening — EMERGENCY:**
- Silent chest (no wheeze — airflow too low)
- Drowsy, confused, or exhausted
- Cyanosis (blue lips)
- SpO2 <85%

**Treatment.**
**Acute Attack — Immediate:**
1. Sit patient upright
2. **Salbutamol inhaler:** 4-8 puffs via spacer (shake, 1 puff, 4 breaths, repeat)
   - If no spacer: use a plastic bottle with hole cut for inhaler
   - If no inhaler: nebulizer salbutamol 2.5-5 mg
3. Repeat every 20 minutes for up to 3 doses
4. If improving: continue salbutamol every 4 hours for 24-48 hours
5. If NOT improving after 3 doses: **REFER URGENTLY**
6. Give prednisolone 1-2 mg/kg (max 40 mg) if moderate/severe

**Long-Term Control:**
- **Inhaled corticosteroid (ICS):** Beclomethasone 200-400 mcg twice daily
- Use EVERY DAY even when feeling well (prevents attacks)
- Rinse mouth after each use (prevents oral thrush)
- **Reliever:** Salbutamol inhaler — use only when needed for symptoms

**Trigger Avoidance:**
- No smoking (patient or household members)
- Reduce dust: damp-mop floors, wash bedding weekly in hot water
- Avoid known triggers (smoke, strong smells, cold air)
- Cover nose/mouth in cold weather

**Danger Signs — Refer Immediately.**
- Cannot speak full sentences
- Drowsy or confused during attack
- Not responding to 3 doses of salbutamol
- Blue lips or fingertips
- Silent chest (no wheeze heard)
- Severe attack in pregnancy

Source: WHO PEN for Primary Care (2020); GINA Global Strategy for Asthma Management (2023)
