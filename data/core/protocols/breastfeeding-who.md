# WHO: Breastfeeding Support for Community Health Workers

## Key Recommendations
- Initiate breastfeeding within **first hour** of birth
- **Exclusive breastfeeding** for first 6 months — no water, no formula, no other foods
- Continue breastfeeding with complementary foods up to 2 years or beyond
- Feed **on demand** — whenever baby shows hunger cues (at least 8-12 times/day)

## Signs of Good Attachment
- Mouth wide open
- Lower lip turned outward
- Chin touching breast
- More areola visible above than below mouth
- Slow, deep sucks with swallowing sounds
- No clicking sounds
- No pain for mother (after initial latching)

## Signs Baby Is Getting Enough Milk
- 6+ wet diapers per day (after day 4)
- Regular soft yellow stools
- Baby is alert and active
- Gaining weight (regains birth weight by day 10-14)
- Breast feels softer after feeding

## Common Problems and Solutions

### Sore/Cracked Nipples
- **Cause:** Almost always poor attachment
- **Fix:** Correct positioning — baby's mouth should cover most of areola
- Apply expressed breastmilk to nipples after feeding (natural healing)
- Do NOT use soap on nipples
- Continue breastfeeding — stopping makes it worse

### Engorgement (hard, painful, swollen breasts)
- Common in days 3-5 after birth
- **Fix:** Feed frequently, apply warm compress before feeding to help flow
- Express a small amount by hand if too full for baby to latch
- Cold compress after feeding for comfort

### Perceived Low Milk Supply
- Most common reason is NOT actually low supply — it is infrequent feeding
- **Fix:** Feed more often (every 2-3 hours), both breasts each time
- Ensure good attachment
- Mother should drink plenty of fluids and eat well
- Milk supply = demand-driven (more feeding = more milk)

### Mastitis (breast infection)
- Red, hot, painful area on breast + fever
- **Treatment:**
  - Continue breastfeeding (NOT dangerous for baby — important to empty breast)
  - Frequent feeding from affected breast
  - Warm compress before feeding
  - **Amoxicillin-clavulanate** 625 mg twice daily for 7 days
  - Paracetamol for pain and fever
  - If abscess develops (fluctuant lump): **REFER** for drainage

## HIV and Breastfeeding
- In settings where formula is NOT safe (no clean water, no refrigeration):
  - **Exclusive breastfeeding is safer** than mixed feeding
  - Mother must be on ART throughout breastfeeding
  - Continue breastfeeding for 12-24 months
- Mixed feeding (breast + other foods/fluids before 6 months) INCREASES HIV transmission risk
- If formula is safe and affordable: discuss options with mother

## Complementary Feeding (from 6 months)
- Introduce soft, mashed foods alongside breastmilk
- Start with 2-3 times/day, increase to 3-4 times + snacks by 12 months
- Include: grains, vegetables, fruits, eggs, meat, legumes
- Continue breastfeeding on demand

## When Breastfeeding Is NOT Recommended
- Mother on certain medications (chemotherapy, radioactive drugs)
- Infant with galactosemia (rare metabolic disorder)
- Active untreated TB: express and give by cup until 2 weeks of treatment complete

## Danger Signs — Refer
- Baby not feeding at all or very weak suck
- Baby losing weight after day 5
- Less than 6 wet diapers per day after day 4
- Severe breast abscess (hot, fluctuant, mother very ill)
- Mother too sick to breastfeed (arrange expressed milk or safe alternative)

Source: WHO Recommendations on Breastfeeding (2023); WHO/UNICEF Baby-Friendly Hospital Initiative; WHO HIV and Infant Feeding Guidelines (2016)
