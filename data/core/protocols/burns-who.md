# WHO: Burns Management for Community Health Workers

**Classification by Depth.**
**Superficial (1st degree):**
- Red, painful, no blisters
- Like sunburn
- Heals in 3-7 days without scarring

**Partial Thickness (2nd degree):**
- Red, painful, WET, blisters
- Very painful to touch
- Heals in 2-3 weeks, may scar

**Full Thickness (3rd degree):**
- White, brown, or black
- Dry, leathery
- NO pain (nerve endings destroyed)
- Requires hospital care — will NOT heal without surgery

**Estimating Burn Size (Rule of 9s — Adults).**
- Head: 9%
- Each arm: 9%
- Front of torso: 18%
- Back of torso: 18%
- Each leg: 18%
- Genitals: 1%

**Children:** Child's palm (including fingers) = ~1% body surface area. Count palms to estimate.

**Immediate First Aid (first 20 minutes).**
1. **STOP the burning:** Remove from heat source, remove burning clothing
2. **COOL the burn:** Run cool (not ice cold) clean water over the burn for 20 minutes
   - Effective up to 3 hours after injury
   - Do NOT use ice — causes frostbite injury
3. **Cover:** Apply clean, non-stick dressing or clean cloth
4. **Pain:** Give paracetamol (NOT aspirin in children)

**Do NOT:**
- Do NOT apply toothpaste, butter, oil, egg, flour, or traditional remedies
- Do NOT break blisters
- Do NOT remove clothing stuck to the burn
- Do NOT apply tight bandages

**When to Refer — URGENT.**
Refer immediately if ANY of these:
- Burns >10% body surface area (>5% in children under 5)
- Full thickness (3rd degree) burns of any size
- Burns to face, hands, feet, genitals, or joints
- Circumferential burns (going all the way around a limb)
- Chemical burns
- Electrical burns
- Burns with inhalation injury (fire in enclosed space, singed facial hair, soot in nose/mouth)
- Burns in children under 1 year (any significant burn)

**Treatment at Community Level (minor burns <10%, superficial/partial thickness).**
**Wound Care:**
1. Clean with clean water or saline
2. Apply silver sulfadiazine cream 1% (if available) or petroleum jelly
3. Cover with non-stick dressing
4. Change dressing every 1-2 days
5. Watch for infection signs

**Pain Management:**
- Paracetamol: weight-based dosing, every 6 hours as needed
- Ibuprofen: can add if paracetamol not sufficient (not in children <6 months)

**Nutrition:**
- Burns increase calorie and protein needs significantly
- Encourage high-protein diet: eggs, milk, beans, meat
- Extra fluids to prevent dehydration

**Signs of Burn Infection — Refer.**
- Increasing pain after day 2 (should be decreasing)
- Increasing redness around burn edges
- Pus or foul-smelling discharge
- Fever
- Red streaks spreading from burn

**Fluid Resuscitation (at facility, for large burns).**
**Parkland Formula:** 4 mL × body weight (kg) × % burn area
- Give half in first 8 hours, second half over next 16 hours
- Use Ringer's Lactate or Normal Saline

**Prevention.**
- Keep children away from cooking fires and hot liquids
- Turn pot handles inward on stoves
- Store matches and lighters out of children's reach
- Test bathwater temperature before bathing children
- Keep hot liquids away from table edges
- Install smoke detectors if possible

Source: WHO Emergency Triage Assessment and Treatment (ETAT+); WHO Basic Emergency Care (2018); MSF Clinical Guidelines — Burns chapter
