# WHO: Cardiovascular Emergencies for Community Health Workers

**Heart Attack (Acute Myocardial Infarction).**
**Recognize:**
- Crushing/squeezing chest pain lasting >20 minutes
- Pain may radiate to left arm, jaw, neck, back, or upper abdomen
- Sweating, nausea, dizziness
- Shortness of breath
- Pale or grey skin
- Anxiety, sense of doom
- In women/elderly/diabetics: may present atypically — fatigue, nausea, jaw pain, back pain WITHOUT classic chest pain

**Immediate Actions — Every Minute Counts:**
1. Keep patient calm, seated or semi-reclined
2. **Aspirin 300 mg** — chew, do NOT swallow whole (thins blood, reduces clot)
3. **Nitroglycerin** 0.5 mg sublingual if available and systolic BP >90 (can repeat x3, 5 min apart)
4. Loosen tight clothing
5. If oxygen available: give oxygen
6. Do NOT give anything to eat or drink
7. **REFER URGENTLY** — time to treatment determines survival
8. If patient becomes unconscious and stops breathing: start CPR immediately

**Stroke (Cerebrovascular Accident).**
**Recognize — FAST Test:**
- **F**ace: Ask to smile — one side droops?
- **A**rms: Raise both arms — one drifts down?
- **S**peech: Repeat a sentence — slurred or confused?
- **T**ime: If ANY of these → **REFER IMMEDIATELY**

**Additional Signs:**
- Sudden severe headache ("worst headache of my life")
- Sudden vision loss in one or both eyes
- Sudden difficulty walking, loss of balance
- Sudden numbness/weakness on one side of body
- Confusion, difficulty understanding speech

**Immediate Actions:**
1. **REFER URGENTLY** — treatment within 3-4.5 hours saves brain tissue
2. Note the exact TIME symptoms started (critical for hospital treatment)
3. Position: if conscious, elevate head 30 degrees; if unconscious, recovery position
4. Do NOT give aspirin (could be hemorrhagic stroke — aspirin would worsen bleeding)
5. Do NOT give food or water (swallowing may be impaired)
6. Monitor breathing — be ready for CPR

**Heart Failure (Acute Decompensation).**
**Recognize:**
- Severe difficulty breathing, worse lying down
- Unable to lie flat (must sit upright to breathe)
- Swollen legs, ankles, abdomen
- Rapid heartbeat
- Cough with pink/frothy sputum
- History of heart disease, hypertension, or previous heart failure

**Immediate Actions:**
1. Sit patient upright with legs hanging down (reduces fluid return to lungs)
2. If oxygen available: give oxygen
3. **Furosemide** 40 mg oral or IV if available (removes excess fluid)
4. **REFER URGENTLY** to hospital

**Cardiac Arrest — CPR.**
**Recognize:**
- Person collapses, unresponsive
- Not breathing or only gasping
- No pulse (check carotid for max 10 seconds)

**Start CPR Immediately:**
1. Call for help
2. Place on firm flat surface
3. Hands center of chest, arms straight
4. Push hard and fast: 5-6 cm depth, 100-120 per minute
5. 30 compressions, then 2 rescue breaths
6. Do NOT stop until: patient responds, help arrives, or you are exhausted
7. If AED available: follow voice prompts, shock if advised

**Hypertensive Crisis (BP ≥180/120 with symptoms).**
**Recognize:**
- Very high blood pressure PLUS:
- Severe headache, vision changes
- Chest pain, shortness of breath
- Confusion, nosebleed
- Nausea, vomiting

**Immediate Actions:**
1. Keep patient calm and seated
2. If on BP medication: give their usual dose
3. **Nifedipine** 10 mg oral (bite and swallow, not sublingual) if available
4. **REFER URGENTLY**
5. Recheck BP every 15 minutes

**Prevention Messages.**
- Take blood pressure medications every day, even when feeling well
- Low-salt diet (<5g/day), exercise, healthy weight
- Stop smoking — biggest modifiable risk factor
- Control diabetes — check blood sugar regularly
- Limit alcohol
- Know your numbers: BP, blood sugar, weight
- Seek care for chest pain or sudden neurological symptoms IMMEDIATELY — do not wait

Source: WHO HEARTS Technical Package (2018); WHO PEN for Primary Care; WHO Basic Emergency Care (2018); AHA/ESC Acute Coronary Syndrome Guidelines
