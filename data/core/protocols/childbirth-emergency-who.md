# WHO: Childbirth Emergencies for Community Health Workers

**Normal Delivery Signs.**
- Regular contractions increasing in frequency
- Cervix dilating (checked by trained birth attendant)
- Urge to push when fully dilated
- Baby's head visible at vaginal opening

**Emergencies BEFORE Delivery.**
**Antepartum Hemorrhage (bleeding before delivery):**
- ANY vaginal bleeding after 20 weeks = **REFER URGENTLY**
- Lay on left side, elevate legs
- Start IV if available
- Do NOT do vaginal examination (could worsen bleeding)

**Prolonged/Obstructed Labor:**
- Active labor >12 hours (or >1 hour pushing without progress)
- Signs: mother exhausted, swollen vulva, blood in urine
- **REFER URGENTLY** — risk of uterine rupture

**Cord Prolapse:**
- Umbilical cord visible or felt at vagina before baby
- **EMERGENCY:**
  1. Place mother in knee-chest position (on knees, chest on floor, buttocks elevated)
  2. Push baby's head UP and away from cord with gloved hand — do NOT let go
  3. **REFER URGENTLY** — baby needs immediate cesarean
  4. Keep cord moist with warm saline-soaked cloth

**Emergencies AFTER Delivery.**
**Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH) — #1 Killer of Mothers:**
Heavy bleeding after delivery (>500 mL or soaking >1 pad in 5 minutes)

**Immediate actions:**
1. **Massage the uterus:** Place hand on abdomen, rub uterus firmly in circular motion until it feels hard like a ball
2. **Empty the bladder** — full bladder prevents uterus from contracting
3. **Oxytocin** 10 IU IM (if available) — causes uterus to contract
4. **Misoprostol** 800 mcg sublingual if oxytocin not available
5. **IV fluids** — run fast, Normal Saline or Ringer's Lactate
6. **REFER URGENTLY** — do NOT stop uterine massage during transport
7. Keep rubbing uterus until firm and bleeding stops

**Retained Placenta:**
- Placenta not delivered within 30 minutes of baby
- Do NOT pull on the cord
- Give Oxytocin 10 IU IM
- **REFER** for manual removal

**Perineal Tears:**
- Minor (1st degree): clean, may heal without stitches
- Moderate to severe (2nd-4th degree): **REFER** for repair
- Apply pressure with clean cloth to control bleeding

**Postpartum Danger Signs — Refer.**
- Heavy bleeding (soaking pad in <1 hour)
- Fever >38°C after delivery (puerperal sepsis)
- Foul-smelling vaginal discharge
- Severe abdominal pain
- Difficulty breathing
- Convulsions (postpartum eclampsia — can occur up to 48 hours after delivery)
- Severe headache with visual changes

Source: WHO Managing Complications in Pregnancy and Childbirth (MCPC); WHO Recommendations for PPH Prevention and Treatment (2023)
