# WHO: Choking (Foreign Body Airway Obstruction) for Community Health Workers

**Recognition.**
- Person suddenly unable to speak, cough, or breathe
- May clutch throat (universal choking sign)
- Skin turns blue (cyanosis) if airway fully blocked
- In children: sudden onset while eating or playing with small objects

**Mild Obstruction (can cough).**
- Encourage the person to keep coughing
- Do NOT interfere — coughing is the most effective way to clear the airway
- Stay with the person and monitor
- If coughing becomes ineffective → treat as severe obstruction

**Severe Obstruction (cannot cough, speak, or breathe).**
**Adults and Children >1 year:**
1. **Back blows:** Stand behind the person, lean them forward. Give 5 sharp blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand
2. **Abdominal thrusts (Heimlich maneuver):** Stand behind the person, place fist above navel, grasp with other hand, pull sharply inward and upward. Give 5 thrusts
3. **Alternate:** 5 back blows → 5 abdominal thrusts → repeat until object is expelled or person becomes unconscious

**Infants <1 year:**
1. **Back blows:** Hold infant face-down on your forearm, support head. Give 5 back blows between shoulder blades
2. **Chest thrusts:** Turn infant face-up, give 5 chest thrusts (2 fingers on lower sternum, same position as CPR)
3. **Alternate:** 5 back blows → 5 chest thrusts → repeat
4. **NEVER** do abdominal thrusts on infants — risk of organ damage

**Pregnant Women or Obese Patients:**
- Use chest thrusts instead of abdominal thrusts
- Place fist on center of chest (sternum), thrust inward

**If Person Becomes Unconscious.**
1. Lower person to ground carefully
2. Call for help / arrange referral
3. Open mouth — look for visible object, remove if seen (finger sweep only if object visible)
4. Begin CPR: 30 chest compressions, 2 rescue breaths
5. Check mouth after each cycle for dislodged object

**Prevention.**
- Cut food into small pieces for young children
- Supervise children while eating — no running or laughing with food
- Keep small objects (coins, buttons, batteries, nuts) away from children <3 years
- Grapes, hot dogs, hard candy are high-risk choking foods — cut lengthwise

**Refer Immediately.**
- Object not cleared after repeated attempts
- Person loses consciousness
- Breathing difficulty persists after object removed (possible airway injury)
- Suspected object still in airway (persistent cough, drooling, pain swallowing)

Source: WHO First Aid Guidelines (2016); AHA/ILCOR Guidelines for CPR and Emergency Cardiovascular Care (2020)
