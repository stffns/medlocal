# WHO: Cholera Management for Community Health Workers

**When to Suspect Cholera.**
- Sudden onset of profuse watery diarrhea ("rice water" stool)
- Severe dehydration developing within hours
- Vomiting
- No fever (or mild)
- During a known cholera outbreak in the area
- Any person ≥2 years with acute watery diarrhea during an outbreak = suspect cholera

**Classification by Dehydration.**
**No Dehydration:**
- Alert, drinks normally, skin pinch normal

**Some Dehydration:**
- Restless/irritable, thirsty, sunken eyes, slow skin pinch

**Severe Dehydration — EMERGENCY:**
- Lethargic/unconscious, unable to drink, very slow skin pinch
- **Can die within hours without treatment**

**Treatment.**
**No/Some Dehydration — Treat at Oral Rehydration Point:**
1. ORS solution: drink as much as tolerated
2. For some dehydration: ORS 75 mL/kg over 4 hours
3. Zinc: 20 mg/day for 10 days (children); adults do not need zinc
4. Continue feeding
5. Monitor for worsening

**Severe Dehydration — URGENT:**
1. **IV fluids immediately:** Ringer's Lactate
   - Adults: 100 mL/kg over 3 hours (30 mL/kg in first 30 min)
   - Children: 100 mL/kg over 6 hours (30 mL/kg in first hour)
2. Reassess every 15-30 minutes
3. Begin ORS as soon as patient can drink
4. **Antibiotic** (shortens duration, reduces transmission):
   - Adults: Doxycycline 300 mg single dose
   - Children: Azithromycin 20 mg/kg single dose
   - Pregnant women: Azithromycin 1g single dose

**Prevention During Outbreak.**
- Drink only treated water (boil, chlorinate, or filter)
- Wash hands with soap after toilet, before eating/cooking
- Cook food thoroughly, eat while hot
- Use latrines — do NOT defecate in open
- Oral cholera vaccine if available (2 doses, 2 weeks apart)

**Danger Signs — Refer Immediately.**
- Unable to drink or keep fluids down
- Lethargy or unconsciousness
- Sunken eyes with no tears
- Very fast weak pulse
- Cold extremities
- No urine for 6+ hours

Source: WHO Cholera Treatment Guidelines (2023); WHO Disease Outbreak Response Handbook
