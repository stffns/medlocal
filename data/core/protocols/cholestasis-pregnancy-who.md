# Intrahepatic Cholestasis of Pregnancy (ICP) — Recognition and Referral

Also known as: colestasis intrahepática del embarazo, obstetric cholestasis, pruritus gravidarum, picazón en el embarazo.

**When to Suspect ICP — Red Flag Pattern.**
- **Intense itching on palms of hands and soles of feet** — this is the hallmark symptom. Itching specifically on palms/soles during pregnancy is NOT normal pregnancy discomfort. It is a red flag for cholestasis until proven otherwise.
- Itching worse at night, may disturb sleep
- Usually starts after week 28-30 (third trimester)
- NO rash — only itching (scratch marks may be present from scratching)
- May have dark urine, pale stools, mild jaundice (uncommon)
- General body itching in pregnancy CAN be benign, but palms/soles itching MUST be investigated

**Why It Matters — Fetal Risk.**
- ICP is associated with stillbirth, especially when serum bile acids exceed 40 µmol/L
- Stillbirth risk increases significantly when bile acids reach 100 µmol/L (3.44% incidence)
- Also associated with: premature birth, fetal distress, meconium aspiration, respiratory distress syndrome in the newborn
- Risk is to the FETUS, not the mother — the mother's symptoms resolve after delivery

**CHW Action — ALWAYS REFER.**
1. **Do NOT dismiss itching on palms/soles in pregnancy as normal.** This requires blood tests (serum bile acids, liver function tests) that are outside CHW scope.
2. **REFER to antenatal care or hospital** for bile acid measurement and liver function tests.
3. **Tell the patient:** "Itching on your hands and feet during pregnancy needs a blood test to check your liver. This is important for your baby's safety."
4. **Urgency:** Refer within 24-48 hours. If the patient also has jaundice (yellow eyes/skin), dark urine, or reduced fetal movement — REFER IMMEDIATELY.
5. **Do NOT prescribe antihistamines or creams** — they do not help ICP itching and may delay proper diagnosis.
6. **Monitoring while waiting for referral:** Ask about fetal movement — reduced movement is an emergency.

**What the Hospital Will Do (for CHW awareness, NOT CHW scope).**
- Measure serum bile acids (diagnostic: >10 µmol/L, severe: >40 µmol/L)
- Start ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) — reduces itching and improves fetal outcomes
- Monitor bile acids regularly
- Plan early delivery (36-37 weeks in severe cases)

**Differential — Other Causes of Pregnancy Itching.**
- Pruritic urticarial papules and plaques (PUPPP): itchy rash in stretch marks, NOT on palms/soles — benign
- Eczema flare: history of atopic disease, responds to moisturizers
- Scabies: burrows, interdigital, partner also itchy
- Preeclampsia: itching with facial swelling, headache, visual changes, high BP — EMERGENCY
