# WHO: Dengue Management for Community Health Workers

**When to Suspect Dengue.**
- High fever (40°C/104°F) lasting 2-7 days
- PLUS two or more of: severe headache, pain behind eyes, muscle/joint pain, nausea/vomiting, rash, minor bleeding (gums, nose)
- Lives in or traveled to dengue-endemic area
- Rainy season (mosquito breeding)

**Phases of Dengue.**
**Febrile Phase (days 1-3):**
- High fever, headache, body aches
- May look like malaria or flu
- **Do RDT for malaria to rule out** — treat malaria if positive

**Critical Phase (days 3-7) — MOST DANGEROUS:**
- Fever drops but patient may worsen
- Plasma leakage can cause shock
- Watch for warning signs (see below)

**Recovery Phase (days 7-10):**
- Appetite returns, rash may appear
- Fluid reabsorption — watch for fluid overload

**Treatment (no specific antiviral — supportive care only).**
**Dengue WITHOUT Warning Signs — Treat at Home:**
1. **Paracetamol** for fever and pain (10-15 mg/kg every 6 hours)
2. **DO NOT give aspirin, ibuprofen, or diclofenac** (increases bleeding risk)
3. Encourage fluids: ORS, juices, soups, clean water
4. Rest
5. **Daily follow-up** during critical phase (days 3-7)
6. Monitor platelet count if lab available

**Dengue WITH Warning Signs — REFER to Hospital:**
Warning signs (any one = refer):
- Abdominal pain or tenderness
- Persistent vomiting (≥3 times in 24 hours)
- Fluid accumulation (swollen abdomen, difficulty breathing)
- Mucosal bleeding (gums, nose, vaginal)
- Lethargy or restlessness
- Liver enlargement >2 cm
- Rising hematocrit with falling platelets

**Severe Dengue — EMERGENCY:**
- Severe plasma leakage → shock (cold hands, weak pulse, low BP)
- Severe bleeding (vomiting blood, black stool)
- Severe organ damage (liver, heart, brain)
- **REFER URGENTLY** — needs IV fluids and monitoring

**Prevention.**
- Eliminate standing water (tires, pots, containers) — mosquito breeding sites
- Use mosquito nets and repellents
- Wear long sleeves and pants, especially dawn and dusk
- Dengue mosquito (Aedes) bites during DAYTIME (unlike malaria mosquito)

**Key Differences from Malaria.**
| Feature | Dengue | Malaria |
|---------|--------|---------|
| Mosquito | Aedes (daytime) | Anopheles (nighttime) |
| Fever pattern | Continuous high | Cyclical/intermittent |
| Pain | Severe muscle/joint | Less prominent |
| Bleeding risk | Yes (don't give aspirin) | Rare |
| Treatment | Supportive only | ACT antimalarials |

Source: WHO Dengue Guidelines for Diagnosis, Treatment, Prevention and Control (2009, updated 2023)
