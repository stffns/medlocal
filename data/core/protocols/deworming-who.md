# WHO: Deworming for Community Health Workers

## When to Deworm
- Routine preventive treatment in endemic areas (>20% prevalence of soil-transmitted helminths)
- Symptoms suggesting worm infection: abdominal pain, diarrhea, blood in stool, visible worms in stool, anal itching (pinworm), pale/anemic, poor growth in children, fatigue

## Target Populations for Mass Deworming
- Pre-school children (12-59 months)
- School-age children (5-14 years)
- Pregnant women (after first trimester)
- Adolescent girls
- Frequency: every 6 months in high-prevalence areas, annually in moderate

## Treatment

### Albendazole (preferred)
- Children 12-23 months: 200 mg single dose
- Children ≥24 months, adolescents, adults: 400 mg single dose
- Pregnant women: 400 mg single dose (safe after first trimester)
- Can be chewed or swallowed with water
- Can give with or without food

### Mebendazole (alternative)
- Children ≥24 months, adolescents, adults: 500 mg single dose
- Or 100 mg twice daily for 3 days (for heavy infections)

### Specific Infections
- **Pinworm (Enterobius):** Albendazole 400 mg, repeat in 2 weeks. Treat entire household.
- **Strongyloides:** Ivermectin 200 mcg/kg single dose for 2 days
- **Tapeworm:** Praziquantel 5-10 mg/kg single dose
- **Schistosomiasis:** Praziquantel 40 mg/kg single dose

## Side Effects (mild, temporary)
- Abdominal pain, nausea, diarrhea — from dying worms, not the drug
- May see worms in stool after treatment (normal)
- Rare: dizziness, headache

## Prevention and Hygiene
- Wash hands with soap before eating and after toilet
- Use latrines — do not defecate in open
- Wear shoes/sandals outdoors (hookworm enters through feet)
- Wash fruits and vegetables before eating
- Boil or filter drinking water
- Cook meat thoroughly (prevents tapeworm)

## When NOT to Give Deworming Medication
- First trimester of pregnancy
- Child under 12 months (albendazole) or 24 months (mebendazole)
- Severely ill child — treat illness first, deworm after recovery

Source: WHO Guideline on Preventive Chemotherapy for Soil-Transmitted Helminthiases (2017, updated 2023)
