# WHO: Diabetes Management for Community Health Workers

## When to Suspect Diabetes
- Frequent urination (polyuria)
- Excessive thirst (polydipsia)
- Unexplained weight loss
- Blurred vision
- Slow-healing wounds or frequent infections
- Tingling or numbness in hands/feet
- Family history of diabetes + obesity

## Diagnosis
- **Fasting blood glucose:** ≥126 mg/dL (≥7.0 mmol/L) — diabetes
- **Random blood glucose:** ≥200 mg/dL (≥11.1 mmol/L) with symptoms — diabetes
- **Pre-diabetes:** Fasting 100-125 mg/dL (5.6-6.9 mmol/L)
- Confirm with a second test on a different day

## Type 2 Diabetes Treatment (most common in adults)

### Lifestyle Modifications (first-line for all)
1. Diet: reduce sugar and refined carbohydrates, increase vegetables and whole grains
2. Physical activity: 30 minutes of moderate exercise most days
3. Weight loss: even 5-10% reduction improves glucose control
4. Stop smoking
5. Limit alcohol

### Oral Medications
**First-line:** Metformin
- Start 500 mg once daily with meals
- Increase gradually to 500 mg twice daily, then up to 1000 mg twice daily
- Side effects: nausea, diarrhea (usually temporary)
- Contraindicated if kidney disease (eGFR <30)

**Second-line (if metformin not sufficient):**
- Glibenclamide 2.5-5 mg daily (risk of hypoglycemia — warn patient)
- Or Glimepiride 1-4 mg daily

### Insulin
- Required for Type 1 diabetes (always)
- Required for Type 2 when oral medications fail
- NPH insulin: start 10 units at bedtime, adjust by 2 units every 3 days
- Store insulin in a cool place (2-8°C if possible, avoid freezing and direct heat)

## Blood Glucose Monitoring Targets
- Fasting: 80-130 mg/dL (4.4-7.2 mmol/L)
- 2 hours after meals: <180 mg/dL (<10.0 mmol/L)
- Check at every clinic visit (at minimum every 3 months)

## Hypoglycemia (Low Blood Sugar) — EMERGENCY
Symptoms: sweating, trembling, confusion, rapid heartbeat, hunger, dizziness

### Treatment (Rule of 15)
1. Give 15g fast sugar: 3-4 sugar cubes, half glass of juice, or 1 tablespoon honey
2. Wait 15 minutes
3. Recheck — if still symptomatic, repeat
4. Once resolved: eat a snack with protein/carbohydrate
5. If unconscious: do NOT give anything by mouth → place on side → **REFER URGENTLY**

## Complications — Screen and Prevent
- **Feet:** Check feet at every visit — ulcers, wounds, numbness, color changes
- **Eyes:** Refer for eye exam annually (diabetic retinopathy)
- **Kidneys:** Check urine for protein annually
- **Blood pressure:** Target <140/90, treat with enalapril/amlodipine if elevated
- **Cardiovascular:** Low-dose aspirin 75-100 mg if age >50 with risk factors

## Foot Care for Diabetics
1. Wash feet daily, dry between toes
2. Check feet for cuts, blisters, red spots daily
3. Never walk barefoot
4. Wear comfortable, closed shoes
5. Cut toenails straight across
6. Do NOT use hot water bottles or heating pads on feet
7. Any wound on diabetic foot → clean, dress, and refer within 24 hours

## Danger Signs — Refer Immediately
- Blood glucose >300 mg/dL (>16.7 mmol/L) with vomiting or confusion (diabetic ketoacidosis)
- Unconscious or unable to eat/drink (severe hypoglycemia)
- Foot ulcer with redness, swelling, pus, or bad smell
- Sudden vision loss
- Chest pain or sudden weakness on one side (stroke)

Source: WHO Package of Essential NCD Interventions (WHO PEN) for Primary Care (2020, updated 2023)
