# WHO: Diarrhea Management in Adults and Adolescents

**Classification.**
**Acute Watery Diarrhea (<14 days):**
- Most common — usually viral, self-limiting
- Main risk: dehydration

**Bloody Diarrhea (Dysentery):**
- Blood and/or mucus in stool
- Fever, abdominal cramps
- Causes: Shigella, Salmonella, Campylobacter, E. coli, Entamoeba

**Persistent Diarrhea (≥14 days):**
- Needs investigation — may indicate HIV, parasites, or inflammatory bowel disease

**Dehydration Assessment in Adults.**
- **No dehydration:** Thirsty, normal pulse, skin pinch normal
- **Some dehydration:** Very thirsty, dry mouth, reduced urine, rapid pulse
- **Severe dehydration:** Unable to drink, very weak pulse, sunken eyes, very dry mouth, little/no urine

**Treatment.**
**Acute Watery Diarrhea — No/Some Dehydration:**
1. **ORS solution:** Drink after each loose stool — 200-400 mL per episode
2. Continue eating: regular diet, avoid very spicy or fatty foods
3. Increase fluids: water, soups, fruit juice (diluted)
4. **No routine antibiotics** — most cases are viral
5. **NO anti-diarrheal drugs** (loperamide) if fever or bloody stool

**Severe Dehydration — URGENT:**
1. **IV fluids:** Ringer's Lactate 1 liter in first 30 minutes, then reassess
2. Continue IV until patient can drink
3. Begin ORS as soon as possible

**Bloody Diarrhea (Dysentery):**
- **Ciprofloxacin** 500 mg twice daily for 3 days
- ORS to prevent dehydration
- If not improving in 48 hours: consider Metronidazole 750 mg 3x daily for 5 days (amoebic dysentery)
- **Stool sample** if available — look for parasites

**Traveler's Diarrhea:**
- Usually self-limiting (3-5 days)
- ORS + bland diet
- Azithromycin 500 mg single dose (or 3-day course) if severe

**Cholera Considerations.**
If during outbreak or rice-water stool: see cholera protocol

**Food Safety Advice.**
- Boil it, cook it, peel it, or forget it
- Wash hands before eating and after toilet
- Store cooked food properly — eat within 2 hours or refrigerate
- Drink treated/boiled water

**Danger Signs — Refer.**
- Unable to keep fluids down (persistent vomiting)
- Blood in stool with high fever
- Signs of severe dehydration
- Diarrhea >3 days with worsening symptoms
- Elderly, pregnant, or immunocompromised with dehydration

Source: WHO Treatment of Diarrhoeal Disease Guidelines; WHO IMAI Acute Care
