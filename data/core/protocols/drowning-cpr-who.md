# WHO: Drowning and CPR for Community Health Workers

## Drowning — Immediate Rescue
1. **Do NOT enter deep water** unless trained in water rescue
2. Reach with a branch, throw a rope or flotation device
3. Once out of water: place on back on a firm surface

## Check for Breathing (max 10 seconds)
- Look: chest rising?
- Listen: breathing sounds?
- Feel: air from nose/mouth?

## If NOT Breathing — Start CPR Immediately

### Rescue Breaths (priority in drowning — lungs are full of water)
1. Tilt head back, lift chin
2. Pinch nose closed
3. Give 5 rescue breaths — seal your mouth over theirs, blow for 1 second each
4. Watch for chest rise

### Chest Compressions
1. Place heel of one hand on center of chest (between nipples)
2. Place other hand on top, interlock fingers
3. Push hard and fast: 5-6 cm depth, 100-120 compressions/minute
4. Ratio: 30 compressions : 2 breaths
5. Do NOT stop until: patient breathes, help arrives, or you are exhausted

### CPR in Children (1-8 years)
- Use one hand for compressions
- Depth: 5 cm (one third of chest depth)
- Same ratio: 30:2

### CPR in Infants (<1 year)
- Use 2 fingers for compressions (just below nipple line)
- Depth: 4 cm
- Cover mouth AND nose with your mouth for breaths
- Ratio: 30:2

## Recovery Position (if breathing but unconscious)
1. Place on side
2. Top leg bent at knee for stability
3. Top hand under cheek
4. Tilt head back slightly to keep airway open
5. Monitor breathing continuously

## After Resuscitation
- **REFER to hospital** even if fully recovered — risk of delayed pulmonary edema (secondary drowning)
- Keep warm — hypothermia is common
- Watch for vomiting (turn on side)

## Choking

### Adult/Child (conscious)
1. Encourage forceful coughing
2. If cannot cough/speak/breathe:
   - Stand behind, arms around waist
   - Make a fist above navel, below ribcage
   - Pull sharply inward and upward (Heimlich maneuver)
   - Repeat up to 5 times
3. If unconscious: begin CPR, check mouth for object before each breath

### Infant (<1 year)
1. Place face-down on your forearm, head lower than body
2. Give 5 firm back blows between shoulder blades
3. Turn over, give 5 chest thrusts (same location as CPR)
4. Alternate back blows and chest thrusts
5. If unconscious: begin infant CPR

## Prevention
- Never leave children unsupervised near water
- Fence wells, ponds, and water storage
- Teach children to swim
- Use life jackets in boats
- Do not swim after drinking alcohol

Source: WHO Global Report on Drowning Prevention (2014); WHO/ILCOR Resuscitation Guidelines
