# Drug Allergies and Alternative Medications for Community Health Workers

## Paracetamol (Acetaminophen) Allergy
True allergy is rare. If confirmed:
- **For pain:** Ibuprofen 400 mg every 8 hours (if no NSAID allergy and no dengue/bleeding risk)
- **For fever:** Ibuprofen, OR physical cooling (tepid sponging, light clothing, fluids)
- **For children:** Ibuprofen 10 mg/kg every 8 hours (if >6 months)
- **If BOTH paracetamol AND NSAIDs allergic:** Physical cooling only, refer for alternatives (metamizole where available)

## NSAID Allergy (Aspirin, Ibuprofen, Diclofenac)
- **For pain/fever:** Paracetamol (usually safe even in aspirin-allergic patients)
- **For inflammation:** Paracetamol + cold compress
- **AVOID all NSAIDs** if history of asthma + nasal polyps + NSAID reaction (Samter's triad)

## Penicillin/Amoxicillin Allergy
Common allergy. Alternatives by condition:
- **Pneumonia:** Erythromycin 500 mg every 6 hours for 5 days, OR Azithromycin 500 mg day 1 then 250 mg days 2-5
- **Ear infection:** Erythromycin or Azithromycin (same doses)
- **Skin infection:** Erythromycin, OR Clindamycin 300 mg every 8 hours
- **UTI:** Nitrofurantoin 100 mg twice daily (not affected by penicillin allergy)
- **STI (gonorrhea):** Ceftriaxone is usually safe in penicillin-allergic patients (cross-reaction <1%)
- **Syphilis:** If severe penicillin allergy, Doxycycline 100 mg twice daily for 14 days

## Sulfa Drug Allergy (Cotrimoxazole/Sulfadoxine-Pyrimethamine)
- **For UTI:** Amoxicillin-clavulanate or Nitrofurantoin
- **For Pneumocystis prophylaxis (HIV):** Dapsone 100 mg daily
- **For malaria prevention (IPTp):** Cannot use SP — discuss alternatives with supervisor
- **Note:** Cotrimoxazole allergy does NOT mean allergy to sulfonylureas (diabetes drugs)

## Chloroquine Allergy
- **For malaria:** Use any ACT (artemether-lumefantrine, artesunate-amodiaquine)

## Metronidazole Allergy
- **For anaerobic infections:** Clindamycin 300 mg every 8 hours
- **For amoebic dysentery:** Tinidazole (if available), otherwise refer

## General Rules for Drug Allergies
1. ALWAYS ask about allergies BEFORE prescribing
2. Record the allergy clearly on the patient card
3. Ask: "What happened when you took it?" — distinguish true allergy (rash, swelling, breathing difficulty) from side effect (nausea, headache)
4. Side effects are NOT allergies — the drug may still be used with precautions
5. If severe allergy history (anaphylaxis): NEVER re-challenge, use alternative class
6. If unsure about alternative: **REFER** rather than guess

## Cross-Reactivity
- Penicillin → Amoxicillin → Ampicillin: ALL cross-react (same class)
- Penicillin → Cephalosporins (Ceftriaxone): low cross-reaction (~1%), usually safe
- Aspirin → All NSAIDs: may cross-react in aspirin-sensitive asthma
- Sulfa antibiotics → Sulfa non-antibiotics: do NOT cross-react

Source: WHO Essential Medicines List; WHO Model Formulary; MSF Essential Drugs Guide
