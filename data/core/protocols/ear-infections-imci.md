# WHO/IMCI: Ear Infections for Community Health Workers

**Assessment.**
Ask: "Does the child have an ear problem?"
Look and feel: pus draining from ear, ear pain, swelling behind ear

**Classification.**
**Acute Ear Infection:**
- Ear pain (child pulls at ear, cries)
- Pus draining from ear for LESS than 14 days
- Red, bulging eardrum (if otoscope available)

**Chronic Ear Infection:**
- Pus draining from ear for 14 DAYS OR MORE
- Usually painless
- Risk of permanent hearing loss

**Mastoiditis — EMERGENCY:**
- Tender swelling BEHIND the ear
- Ear pushed forward
- **REFER URGENTLY** — can be life-threatening

**Treatment.**
**Acute Ear Infection:**
1. **Amoxicillin** for 5 days:
   - 2-12 months: 250 mg twice daily
   - 1-5 years: 500 mg twice daily
2. **Paracetamol** for pain
3. Dry ear: wick with rolled clean cloth to absorb discharge
4. Do NOT put oil, herbs, or drops in the ear
5. Follow up in 5 days

**Chronic Ear Infection:**
1. **Dry the ear** — this is the most important treatment:
   - Roll clean cloth into a wick
   - Insert gently, remove when wet, repeat until dry
   - Do this 3 times daily
2. **Ciprofloxacin ear drops** (if available): 3 drops twice daily for 2 weeks
3. Do NOT put anything else in the ear
4. Keep water out of the ear when bathing
5. **Refer** if not improving in 2 weeks (may need specialist)

**Ear Infection in a Young Infant (<3 months) — ALWAYS Urgent.**
Any infant under 3 months with an ear complaint (pulling at the ear, pus or fluid from the ear, fever of any degree, irritability, poor feeding with ear findings) must be **REFERRED URGENTLY**. In this age group an ear infection can be the first sign of neonatal or early infant sepsis, and outpatient oral antibiotics are NOT appropriate. Do NOT start oral amoxicillin, do NOT put drops in the ear, do NOT wait for follow-up. Refer to a facility for parenteral evaluation.

**Ear Drops — What Is Safe and What Is Not.**
This is a dangerous area for CHW practice and the single most load-bearing rule is: **never put anything in the ear unless you know the eardrum is intact, and never put an aminoglycoside in an ear you suspect is perforated.** When in doubt, do not drop, refer.

**SAFE topical ear drops (with intact or perforated eardrum):**
- **Ciprofloxacin 0.3 % ear drops** — the preferred agent for chronic discharging ears and for ears with a known or suspected perforation. Fluoroquinolone ear drops do NOT cause ototoxicity when they reach the middle ear through a perforation. 3 drops twice daily for 2 weeks, after drying the ear with a wick as described above.

**DANGEROUS topical ear drops — AVOID:**
- **Aminoglycoside ear drops — gentamicin, neomycin, tobramycin, framycetin** — are **CONTRAINDICATED if perforation is suspected or confirmed**, and should be **avoided under 1 year of age** even without known perforation. Aminoglycosides that reach the middle ear are absorbed by the inner ear and can cause **permanent, irreversible hearing loss and/or balance damage**. The risk is highest in infants and in ears that are already infected and inflamed.
- **Aminoglycoside + steroid combinations** (e.g. Sofradex, Garasone) carry the same aminoglycoside risk and must not be used if perforation is suspected.
- **Oil, herbs, breast milk, or any home remedy in the ear** — do not use.
- **Hydrogen peroxide or alcohol in a pediatric ear** — do not use, irritant.

**Recognition rule — suspected ototoxicity.** A baby or young child who is **not responding to sounds** after receiving aminoglycoside ear drops is a **suspected aminoglycoside ototoxicity case** until proven otherwise. Refer urgently to a facility that can do a formal hearing assessment and an ENT evaluation. Document the drops used, the duration, and the dose. The damage may be permanent but early intervention for hearing and speech support matters.

**Hearing Loss Assessment.**
- Child not responding to sounds
- Speech delay in young child
- Need to repeat or speak loudly
- Recent use of aminoglycoside ear drops or injectable aminoglycosides (gentamicin, amikacin, streptomycin), or loop diuretics, or cisplatin — these are ototoxic drugs
- **Refer** for hearing assessment

**Prevention.**
- Breastfeed exclusively for 6 months (protects against ear infections)
- Keep ears dry — dry after bathing
- Do not insert objects into ear canal
- Treat upper respiratory infections promptly

**Danger Signs — Refer Immediately.**
- Swelling behind the ear (mastoiditis)
- Facial weakness on side of infected ear
- Severe headache with ear infection
- Dizziness/vertigo with ear discharge
- Chronic discharge not responding to 2 weeks of treatment

Source: WHO/UNICEF IMCI Guidelines; WHO Primary Ear and Hearing Care Training Manual (2006)
