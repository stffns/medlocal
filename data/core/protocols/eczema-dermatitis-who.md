# Eczema and Common Skin Conditions for Community Health Workers

## Eczema (Atopic Dermatitis)

### When to Suspect
- Itchy, dry, red, flaky skin
- Common locations: face (infants), inner elbows, behind knees (children/adults)
- Chronic and relapsing — comes and goes
- Often family history of eczema, asthma, or allergies
- Worse with dry weather, irritants (soap, detergent), stress

### Treatment
1. **Moisturize** — most important step. Apply petroleum jelly or coconut oil 2-3 times daily
2. **Avoid triggers** — harsh soaps, synthetic clothing, excessive bathing
3. **For flares (red, itchy, inflamed):**
   - Hydrocortisone cream 1% — apply thinly twice daily for 5-7 days
   - Do NOT use steroid cream on face for more than 5 days
   - Do NOT use strong steroids in children without medical supervision
4. **For itching:** Cetirizine 10mg daily (adults) or 5mg (children 2-6)
5. **If infected** (crusting, pus, spreading redness): add oral antibiotics (amoxicillin-clavulanate)

### Prevention
- Short lukewarm baths (not hot)
- Use mild soap only on dirty areas
- Pat dry, apply moisturizer immediately while skin is still damp
- Cotton clothing, avoid wool and synthetics
- Keep nails short to reduce scratching damage

## Contact Dermatitis

### When to Suspect
- Red, itchy rash in area of contact with irritant
- Clear boundary — rash matches where the substance touched skin
- Common causes: plants, latex gloves, jewelry (nickel), chemicals, detergents

### Treatment
1. Identify and REMOVE the cause
2. Wash affected area with water
3. Hydrocortisone cream 1% twice daily for 5-7 days
4. Cetirizine for itching
5. Usually resolves once exposure stops

## Seborrheic Dermatitis

### When to Suspect
- Yellow, greasy, flaky scales
- Scalp (dandruff), eyebrows, nasolabial folds, behind ears, chest
- In infants: "cradle cap" — thick yellow scales on scalp
- Common in HIV patients — may be first presentation

### Treatment
- Scalp: ketoconazole shampoo 2%, use 2-3 times per week
- Face/body: ketoconazole cream 2% or hydrocortisone 1% twice daily
- Cradle cap: apply mineral oil, gently remove scales with soft brush, then wash
- Recurrent in adults: **offer HIV test** if not previously tested

## Urticaria (Hives)

### When to Suspect
- Raised, itchy, red welts that appear suddenly
- Individual welts last <24 hours (move around)
- Common triggers: food, medication, insect bites, infection
- If face/lips swelling or breathing difficulty → **ANAPHYLAXIS — REFER URGENTLY**

### Treatment
- Cetirizine 10mg daily (may need higher dose: up to 20mg)
- Remove trigger if identified
- Usually resolves in hours to days
- If lasting >6 weeks → chronic urticaria → **refer** for investigation

## Pellagra (Vitamin B3 Deficiency)

### When to Suspect — The 3 Ds
- **Dermatitis:** symmetric red rash on sun-exposed areas (face, neck, hands)
- **Diarrhea**
- **Dementia** (confusion, memory loss)
- Common in: maize/corn-based diets, alcoholism, malnutrition, TB treatment (isoniazid)

### Treatment
- Nicotinamide 300mg daily for 3-4 weeks
- Improve diet: meat, fish, legumes, groundnuts
- If on isoniazid: give pyridoxine (vitamin B6) to prevent

Source: MSF Clinical Guidelines Chapter 4; WHO Essential Medicines; WHO Nutrition Guidelines
