# WHO: Epilepsy Management for Community Health Workers (mhGAP)

**When to Suspect Epilepsy.**
- Two or more unprovoked seizures (not caused by fever, drugs, or acute illness)
- Types of seizures:
  - **Tonic-clonic (grand mal):** Falls, stiffens, jerks all limbs, may bite tongue, may lose bladder control
  - **Absence:** Staring spells, unresponsive for seconds (common in children)
  - **Focal:** Jerking of one arm/leg, unusual sensations, confused behavior

**During a Seizure — First Aid.**
1. Stay calm, note the time
2. Protect from injury — move sharp objects away
3. Turn on side (recovery position) to prevent choking
4. Do NOT put anything in the mouth
5. Do NOT restrain the person
6. Do NOT give water or medicine during the seizure
7. Stay with them until fully conscious
8. If seizure lasts >5 minutes: **EMERGENCY** — give diazepam if available and refer

**Treatment.**
**First-Line Medications (WHO mhGAP):**
- **Phenobarbital** (the most widely available and most used antiepileptic in low-resource settings — WHO Essential Medicines List):
  - Adults: start 60 mg at night, titrate to 90-180 mg daily
  - Children: 3-5 mg/kg/day, single dose at night (sedation is usually tolerated if given at bedtime)
  - Effective for generalized tonic-clonic and focal seizures
  - **Important:** phenobarbital induces hepatic CYP enzymes, which can lower blood levels of some other drugs (oral contraceptives, warfarin, some antiretrovirals). Paracetamol at therapeutic doses is NOT a clinical problem — see Fever section below.
- **Carbamazepine** (preferred for focal seizures):
  - Adults: start 200 mg daily, increase to 200 mg twice daily over 2 weeks
  - Children: 5 mg/kg/day, increase gradually
- **Sodium valproate** (preferred for generalized seizures):
  - Adults: start 300 mg daily, increase to 300 mg twice daily
  - Children: 10-15 mg/kg/day
  - **Do NOT use in women of childbearing age** (causes birth defects)
- **Phenytoin** is also on the WHO EML; narrow therapeutic window, requires level monitoring when possible, refer for initiation.

**Key Rules:**
- Take medication EVERY DAY at the same time
- Never stop suddenly — causes severe seizures (status epilepticus)
- Reduce dose gradually over months if seizure-free for 2+ years
- Avoid alcohol — triggers seizures
- Phenobarbital and other antiepileptics cause drowsiness initially; it usually improves after 2-4 weeks. Warn the patient not to operate machinery until stable.

**Fever in a Patient with Known Epilepsy.**
Fever is a recognised trigger for breakthrough seizures in children and adults on antiepileptic medication. The CHW must do two things at once: treat the fever safely and watch for a seizure.

1. **Treat the fever with paracetamol.** Paracetamol at standard therapeutic doses (10-15 mg/kg every 6 hours in children, 500-1000 mg every 6 hours in adults, maximum 60 mg/kg/day in children and 4 g/day in adults) is **SAFE** with phenobarbital, carbamazepine, valproate, and phenytoin. Do NOT stop the antipyretic because of the antiepileptic. The small theoretical concern about enzyme induction and NAPQI is a high-dose / overdose consideration, not a therapeutic-dose concern.
2. **Do NOT use aspirin** in children under 12 (Reye syndrome, see fever protocol).
3. **Ibuprofen** at normal doses is also compatible with antiepileptics, with the usual age limits (≥6 months) and contraindications (dengue, dehydration, bleeding risk).
4. **Continue the antiepileptic on schedule.** Do not skip doses because the child is sick. If the child is vomiting and cannot keep the medication down, refer immediately for parenteral or rectal dosing.
5. **Watch for breakthrough seizure.** Fever plus epilepsy is a higher-risk combination. Any seizure that occurs in this setting, or a fever that does not respond to antipyretic within 2 hours, or any danger sign from the fever protocol, should trigger urgent referral.
6. **Look for the underlying cause of the fever.** Meningitis, malaria, pneumonia, and UTI all need to be ruled out in a patient on antiepileptics with fever. See the fever assessment protocol.

> **Load-bearing clarification for CHWs:** a parent who has given paracetamol to their child on phenobarbital has done the **right** thing. Reassure them, continue the antipyretic, monitor for seizure, refer for evaluation of the fever source — but do not tell them to stop the paracetamol or the phenobarbital.

**Status Epilepticus — EMERGENCY.**
- Seizure lasting >5 minutes OR repeated seizures without regaining consciousness
1. Ensure airway is open
2. Give **diazepam** rectal: 0.5 mg/kg (max 10 mg) OR IV: 0.15 mg/kg
3. If no diazepam: give **midazolam** buccal (between cheek and gum): 0.2 mg/kg
4. **REFER URGENTLY** to hospital

**Danger Signs — Refer.**
- First seizure ever (needs diagnosis)
- Seizures increasing in frequency despite medication
- Seizure lasting >5 minutes
- Seizure with fever and stiff neck (meningitis)
- Head injury followed by seizure
- Seizure during pregnancy (may be eclampsia)
