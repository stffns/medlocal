# WHO: Eye Infections and Problems for Community Health Workers

**Conjunctivitis (Pink Eye).**
- Red eye, discharge (watery or pus), itchy or gritty feeling
- Usually both eyes affected
- Very contagious — wash hands frequently

**Treatment:**
- Clean eyes: wipe discharge with clean cloth dampened with clean water, from inner to outer corner
- **Tetracycline eye ointment 1%**: apply inside lower eyelid, 3 times daily for 7 days
- If no ointment available: clean eyes frequently and refer
- Do NOT patch the eye (traps bacteria)

**Neonatal Conjunctivitis (within first 28 days) — URGENT.**
- Pus from eyes in a newborn = possible gonorrhea or chlamydia
- Can cause blindness if untreated
1. Clean eyes with saline or clean water
2. **Ceftriaxone** 50 mg/kg IM single dose (max 125 mg) for gonorrhea
3. PLUS **Erythromycin** syrup for 14 days for chlamydia
4. **Treat the mother and partner** for STI
5. **Prevention:** Tetracycline 1% eye ointment at birth for all newborns

**Trachoma.**
- Leading infectious cause of blindness worldwide
- Caused by repeated Chlamydia trachomatis infection
- Signs: follicles on inner upper eyelid, scarring, eyelashes turning inward (trichiasis)

**WHO SAFE Strategy:**
- **S**urgery for trichiasis (refer)
- **A**ntibiotics: Azithromycin 20 mg/kg single dose (mass treatment in endemic areas)
- **F**acial cleanliness: wash face daily with clean water
- **E**nvironmental improvement: latrines, clean water supply

**Foreign Body in Eye.**
1. Do NOT rub the eye
2. Wash hands, look for object
3. Flush with clean water
4. If visible on white part: remove gently with corner of clean damp cloth
5. If on cornea (colored part) or embedded: **DO NOT remove** — patch eye and **REFER**

**Danger Signs — Refer Immediately (possible vision loss).**
- Sudden vision loss or decrease
- Eye pain (not just irritation) — possible corneal ulcer or acute glaucoma
- White spot on cornea (ulcer)
- Eye injury with wound or penetration
- Newborn with pus from eyes
- Eyelashes turning inward and rubbing on eye (trichiasis)
- No improvement after 5 days of treatment

Source: WHO Primary Eye Care Training Manual (2018); WHO SAFE Strategy for Trachoma; WHO Guidelines on Neonatal Conjunctivitis
