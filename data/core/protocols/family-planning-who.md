# WHO: Family Planning for Community Health Workers

## Methods Available at Community Level

### Condoms (Male)
- 98% effective with correct use
- Also prevents STIs and HIV
- Use new condom for each act
- Store away from heat and sharp objects

### Combined Oral Contraceptives (COC) — "The Pill"
- Take 1 pill daily at same time
- Start within first 5 days of period (or anytime if reasonably sure not pregnant)
- If missed 1 pill: take as soon as remembered, continue normally
- If missed 2+ pills: take last missed pill, use condoms for 7 days
- **Do NOT give if:** age >35 and smokes, BP ≥140/90, history of blood clots, migraine with aura, breastfeeding <6 months postpartum

### Progestin-Only Pills (POP) — "Mini Pill"
- Take 1 pill daily, same time (late by >3 hours = missed)
- Safe during breastfeeding
- Safe for women with hypertension or migraine
- May cause irregular bleeding

### Injectable (DMPA — Depo-Provera)
- 150 mg IM every 3 months (12-13 weeks)
- Can give up to 2 weeks late without backup method
- May cause irregular bleeding or no periods (both normal)
- Return to fertility may take 6-12 months after stopping

### Implant (Jadelle, Implanon)
- Inserted under skin of upper arm by trained provider
- Effective 3-5 years depending on type
- Can be removed anytime — fertility returns quickly
- **Refer** for insertion and removal

### Emergency Contraception
- Levonorgestrel 1.5 mg single dose (or 0.75 mg x 2 doses 12 hours apart)
- Effective up to 72 hours after unprotected sex (better earlier)
- NOT an abortion pill — prevents pregnancy, does not end existing pregnancy
- Can be given repeatedly if needed

## When to Refer
- IUD insertion/removal
- Implant insertion/removal
- Tubal ligation or vasectomy request
- Suspected pregnancy with contraceptive in place
- Severe headache or vision changes on COC (stop pills, refer)
- Persistent heavy bleeding on any method

Source: WHO Medical Eligibility Criteria for Contraceptive Use (MEC), 6th Edition (2024); WHO Family Planning Handbook (2018)
