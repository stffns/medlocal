# WHO/IMCI: Fever Assessment and Management

## Approach to Fever in Children Under 5
Fever = temperature ≥37.5°C (axillary) or feels hot to touch.

### Step 1 — Rule Out Dangerous Causes
Check for danger signs FIRST:
- Unable to drink or breastfeed
- Vomits everything
- Convulsions
- Lethargic or unconscious
- Stiff neck
→ ANY danger sign = **REFER URGENTLY**

### Step 2 — Assess for Specific Causes
| If fever PLUS... | Suspect | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Cough + fast breathing | Pneumonia | Amoxicillin, see pneumonia protocol |
| Watery diarrhea | Dehydration risk | ORS + zinc, see diarrhea protocol |
| Malaria-endemic area | Malaria | RDT, treat if positive, see malaria protocol |
| Stiff neck + headache | Meningitis | **REFER URGENTLY** |
| Rash (generalized) | Measles | Vitamin A + refer if complicated |
| Ear pain/discharge | Ear infection | Amoxicillin, see ear protocol |
| Painful urination | UTI | Refer for urine test |

### Step 3 — Fever Without Obvious Cause
- If malaria-endemic: do RDT or treat presumptively
- Give paracetamol for fever ≥38.5°C: 10-15 mg/kg every 6 hours
- Increase fluids — breastmilk, ORS, clean water
- Light clothing — do NOT bundle up
- Follow up in 2 days if fever persists

## Fever in Adults
- Common causes: malaria, respiratory infection, UTI, typhoid
- If fever >5 days: test for malaria, consider typhoid, refer for blood tests
- If fever + confusion/altered consciousness: **REFER URGENTLY** (meningitis, cerebral malaria)

## Measles
- Fever + generalized rash + cough/runny nose/red eyes
- **Vitamin A:** 200,000 IU on day 1 and day 2 (100,000 IU if 6-11 months)
- Complications: pneumonia, diarrhea, encephalitis, blindness
- **Refer** if: mouth ulcers preventing eating, pneumonia, eye involvement, or severe malnutrition

## Typhoid Fever
- Sustained high fever >7 days, headache, abdominal pain, constipation or diarrhea
- Treatment: Ciprofloxacin 500 mg twice daily for 7 days (adults)
- Children: Azithromycin 10-20 mg/kg/day for 7 days
- **Refer** if not improving after 5 days of treatment

## Antipyretic Dosing — Children
- **Paracetamol (first choice):** 10-15 mg/kg every 6 hours (safe from birth)
- **Ibuprofen:** 5-10 mg/kg every 8 hours — **only if ≥6 months old**
  - Under 6 months: do NOT give ibuprofen, use paracetamol only
  - At 6 months: prefer paracetamol; ibuprofen is an option if paracetamol insufficient
  - Do NOT give ibuprofen if dehydrated, vomiting, or suspected dengue
- Can alternate paracetamol and ibuprofen if single agent insufficient (≥6 months only)

## Do NOT Use
- Aspirin in children under 12 (risk of Reye syndrome)
- Ibuprofen in children under 6 months
- Ibuprofen in suspected dengue, dehydration, or bleeding risk
- Cold baths or alcohol sponging (causes shivering, raises core temperature)
- Antibiotics for every fever — most fevers are viral

Source: WHO/UNICEF IMCI Guidelines; WHO Pocket Book of Hospital Care for Children
