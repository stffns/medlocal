# WHO: Retained Foreign Body Aspiration for Community Health Workers

For ACTIVE choking (cannot breathe, speak, or cough), see the Choking
First Aid protocol. This one covers the RETAINED object after the
initial event — the child who partially recovered but still has signs.

**When to Suspect Foreign Body Aspiration.**
Suspect retained airway foreign body when these features are present:

- Sudden onset while **eating small food** (peanuts, corn kernels, seeds,
  grapes, hard candy) or **playing with small objects** (beads, coins,
  button batteries, toy parts)
- **History of a choking episode**: coughing fit, gagging, or transient
  blue colour at the moment of onset
- **No fever, no runny nose** (rules out viral trigger or infection)
- **No prior history** of asthma or wheezing in the child
- Peak age: **6 months to 3 years** (but possible at any age)

**Red Flags — Still Suspect Even If Child Looks Better.**
- **Unilateral wheeze** — wheezing heard on ONE side of the chest only
- **Unilateral decreased breath sounds** or asymmetric chest movement
- Persistent cough or drooling after the episode
- Pointing to throat or refusing to swallow

Bilateral wheeze without a choking history is more likely asthma or a
viral trigger — see the Asthma protocol.

**Critical Rules — Do NOT.**
1. **Do NOT give salbutamol as primary treatment.** A mechanical
   obstruction is not bronchospasm; bronchodilators will not move the
   object and delay definitive care.
2. **Do NOT perform blind finger sweeps** — this can push the object
   deeper. Only remove an object you can clearly see.
3. **Do NOT delay referral** for imaging or observation. Definitive
   diagnosis and treatment is rigid bronchoscopy in hospital.

**Immediate Actions (CHW).**
1. Keep the child calm and upright. Agitation can worsen obstruction.
2. Do NOT attempt back blows or Heimlich in a child who is breathing
   effectively — those are for ACTIVE choking only.
3. Nothing by mouth (NPO) in case surgery is needed.
4. Monitor for deterioration: cyanosis, drooling, inability to speak,
   loss of consciousness. If any of these occur → revert to the
   Choking First Aid protocol immediately.
5. **REFER URGENTLY** to the nearest hospital with paediatric
   bronchoscopy capability. State in the referral note:
   "Suspected retained airway foreign body — requires rigid bronchoscopy".

**Refer Immediately If.**
- Any red flag above (unilateral wheeze, asymmetric findings)
- History of choking even if the child looks well now (silent interval
  is a known risk — objects can shift and re-obstruct)
- Suspected button battery (caustic injury, minutes matter)
- Any respiratory distress, cyanosis, or drooling

Source: WHO Pocket Book of Hospital Care for Children (2nd ed., 2013);
WHO First Aid Guidelines (2016).
