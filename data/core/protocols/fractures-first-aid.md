# WHO: Fractures and Musculoskeletal Injuries for Community Health Workers

## How to Recognize a Fracture
- Severe pain, worse with movement
- Swelling and bruising
- Deformity (limb looks bent or shortened)
- Cannot use or bear weight on the limb
- Grating sensation (crepitus) — do NOT test for this
- Open fracture: bone visible through skin — **EMERGENCY**

## First Aid — General Principles
1. **Do not move the injured part** unnecessarily
2. **Splint** the limb in the position found:
   - Use any rigid material: sticks, boards, rolled newspaper, cardboard
   - Pad between splint and skin with cloth
   - Immobilize the joint ABOVE and BELOW the fracture
   - Tie firmly but not too tight (check circulation: fingers/toes should be pink and warm)
3. **Elevate** the limb if possible (reduces swelling)
4. **Cold compress** (cloth soaked in cold water) for first 24 hours
5. **Pain:** Paracetamol or ibuprofen
6. **REFER** for X-ray and proper treatment

## Specific Fractures

### Open (Compound) Fracture — EMERGENCY
- Bone visible through wound
1. Control bleeding with direct pressure (around the wound, not on the bone)
2. Cover wound with clean damp dressing
3. Splint without pushing bone back in
4. Give first dose of antibiotic: Amoxicillin-clavulanate 625 mg
5. Tetanus prophylaxis
6. **REFER URGENTLY**

### Suspected Spine/Neck Injury — CRITICAL
- After fall from height, road accident, or diving injury
1. Do NOT move the patient unless in danger
2. Keep head, neck, and body aligned
3. Immobilize head with rolled towels/clothing on each side
4. Log-roll if must move (3+ people, keep spine straight)
5. **REFER URGENTLY** — spinal cord damage can cause permanent paralysis

### Dislocations
- Joint looks deformed, extremely painful, cannot move
- Do NOT try to push it back in place
- Splint in the position found
- Ice and elevate
- **REFER** for reduction

## RICE Protocol (Sprains and Strains)
- **R**est — stop using the injured part
- **I**ce — cold compress 20 minutes on, 20 minutes off, for first 48 hours
- **C**ompression — wrap firmly (not too tight) with elastic bandage
- **E**levation — raise above heart level

## Danger Signs — Refer Immediately
- Open fracture (bone visible)
- Suspected spine or neck injury
- Loss of feeling or movement below the injury (nerve damage)
- Fingers/toes turning blue, cold, or numb after splinting (too tight)
- Severe deformity
- Multiple fractures
- Fracture with heavy bleeding

Source: WHO Surgical Care at District Hospital (2003); WHO Basic Emergency Care (2018)
