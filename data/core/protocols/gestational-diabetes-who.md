# WHO: Gestational Diabetes (GDM) & Hyperglycaemia in Pregnancy / Diabetes Gestacional en Embarazo for CHWs

Use this protocol for any pregnant woman / mujer embarazada with the
classic diabetic triad — excessive thirst (polydipsia / mucha sed),
excessive hunger (polyphagia / mucha hambre), and excessive urination
(polyuria / orina mucho / orinando mucho) — at ANY gestational age.
These are the cardinal symptoms of hyperglycaemia in pregnancy and
must NEVER be dismissed as normal pregnancy. This protocol also covers
diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) in pregnancy and the harmful practice of
giving sugary drinks to a hyperglycaemic patient.

For diabetes outside pregnancy see the Diabetes protocol. For
pre-eclampsia see the Pregnancy Danger Signs protocol.

**When to Suspect Gestational Diabetes (Diabetes Gestacional).**
Suspect GDM in any pregnant woman / embarazada, especially after 24
weeks, when ANY of these are present:

- The classic diabetic triad / triada clásica: **polyuria** (peeing
  more than usual / orina mucho / orinando mucho), **polydipsia**
  (excessive thirst / mucha sed / sed todo el tiempo), **polyphagia**
  (excessive hunger / mucha hambre / hambre todo el tiempo). Do NOT
  dismiss these as "normal pregnancy / normal por el embarazo" —
  they are the cardinal symptoms of hyperglycaemia / glucosa alta
  at any gestational age.
- Recurrent vaginal or urinary infections, candidiasis
- Glucosuria on routine antenatal urine dipstick
- Macrosomia (large baby) on examination, polyhydramnios
- Risk factors: BMI ≥30, age ≥35, prior GDM, prior macrosomic baby,
  family history of diabetes, certain ethnicities (South Asian,
  African, Hispanic, Pacific Islander)

A pregnant woman who tells you she "drinks water all the time, eats a
lot, and pees a lot" needs blood glucose testing today, not reassurance.

**⚠ Red Flags — Possible Hyperglycaemic Emergency / DKA.**
DKA in pregnancy is a life-threatening obstetric emergency that can
develop at near-normal glucose levels and rapidly. Suspect it when ANY
of these accompany the triad:

- Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain (often misread as "morning sickness")
- Drowsiness, lethargy, confusion, "just wants to sleep"
- Rapid or deep breathing (Kussmaul respiration), fruity-smelling breath
- Dehydration signs: dry mucous membranes, sunken eyes, low urine output
- Dizziness or fainting on standing

If ANY red flag is present → treat as obstetric emergency.

**Critical Rules — Do NOT.**
1. **Do NOT give sugary drinks, sweet tea, juice, honey, or food
   containing sugar** to a pregnant woman with the diabetic triad,
   even if she feels dizzy or weak. You cannot distinguish hypoglycaemia
   from hyperglycaemia by symptoms alone, and giving sugar to a
   hyperglycaemic patient can precipitate DKA.
2. **Do NOT dismiss the triad as normal pregnancy.** Frequent urination
   alone in late pregnancy can be physiological, but combined with
   excessive thirst and hunger it is pathological.
3. **Do NOT delay referral** to test blood glucose. Point-of-care
   glucose strips at the health post are the right test if available.

**Immediate Actions (CHW).**
1. Measure blood pressure to rule out pre-eclampsia (BP ≥140/90 with
   the triad means BOTH may be present — still refer urgently).
2. If a glucose strip is available, measure capillary blood glucose
   NOW. ≥200 mg/dL (≥11.1 mmol/L) random or ≥126 mg/dL (≥7.0 mmol/L)
   fasting confirms hyperglycaemia.
3. If glucose ≥250 mg/dL OR any DKA red flag → **REFER URGENTLY**
   to a facility that can manage obstetric DKA. Arrange transport now.
4. While waiting for transport: keep the woman lying on her LEFT side,
   sips of plain water (no sugar) only, monitor consciousness and
   breathing, do not give insulin or any other drug at the CHW level.
5. If glucose 140-249 mg/dL with no red flags: refer same day for
   formal GDM workup (oral glucose tolerance test).
6. If no glucose strip available and the triad is present: refer
   same day regardless. Do not guess.

**Refer Immediately If.**
- Any DKA red flag (drowsiness, vomiting, abdominal pain, deep breathing,
  fruity breath, dehydration)
- Random glucose ≥250 mg/dL
- Reduced fetal movements alongside the triad
- BP ≥140/90 with the triad (combined GDM + pre-eclampsia)
- Any altered consciousness

Source: WHO Recommendations on Antenatal Care for a Positive Pregnancy
Experience (2016); WHO Diagnostic Criteria and Classification of
Hyperglycaemia First Detected in Pregnancy (2013); WHO PEN protocol on
Diabetes (2020).
