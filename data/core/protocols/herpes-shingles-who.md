# Herpes Simplex and Herpes Zoster (Shingles) for Community Health Workers

## Herpes Simplex (Cold Sores / Genital Herpes)

### Oral Herpes (Cold Sores)
- Painful small blisters (vesicles) on or around lips
- May have tingling/burning before blisters appear
- Recurrent — comes back in the same area
- Very common, NOT dangerous in most people

### Treatment
- Mild: no treatment needed, heals in 7-10 days
- Painful/frequent: Acyclovir 200mg 5 times daily for 5 days
- Keep blisters clean and dry
- Do NOT kiss or share utensils during outbreak

### Genital Herpes
- Painful blisters/ulcers on genitals
- First episode is usually worst
- Treatment: Acyclovir 400mg 3 times daily for 7 days (first episode)
- Recurrent: Acyclovir 400mg 3 times daily for 5 days
- Use condoms to reduce transmission (but not 100% effective)
- **In pregnancy:** if active lesions at delivery → **REFER** for caesarean (risk of neonatal herpes)

## Herpes Zoster (Shingles)

### When to Suspect
- Painful rash with blisters following a SINGLE NERVE (dermatomal distribution)
- Affects ONE SIDE of the body only (left or right, never crosses midline)
- Often on trunk (like a band), can affect face
- Pain may start 2-3 days BEFORE rash appears
- History of chickenpox (reactivation of the same virus)

### Who Gets Shingles
- Elderly (>=50 years)
- Immunocompromised (HIV, cancer, steroids)
- **In a young person (<40): offer HIV test** — shingles in young adults suggests immunosuppression

### Treatment
- **Acyclovir 800mg 5 times daily for 7 days** — start within 72 hours of rash for best effect
- Paracetamol or ibuprofen for pain
- Keep blisters clean and dry, cover with light dressing
- Calamine lotion for itching
- Patient is contagious (can give chickenpox to people who haven't had it) until all blisters are crusted

### Complications
- **Post-herpetic neuralgia:** pain continues months after rash heals. Treat with amitriptyline 25mg at bedtime.
- **Ophthalmic zoster (eye involvement):** blisters on nose tip (Hutchinson sign) → **REFER URGENTLY** to eye specialist — risk of blindness
- **Disseminated zoster:** widespread blisters beyond one dermatome → **REFER** (may indicate severe immunosuppression)

### Danger Signs — Refer
- Blisters near the eye or on the nose tip
- Rash crossing the midline or widespread
- Severe pain not controlled by paracetamol
- Young patient with shingles — needs HIV testing
- Immunocompromised patient with shingles

Source: WHO Essential Medicines; MSF Clinical Guidelines Chapter 4 — Herpes; WHO HIV/Shingles Guidelines
