# WHO: HIV Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) for Community Health Workers

## What is PEP?
- Antiretroviral medications taken AFTER potential HIV exposure to prevent infection
- Must start within **72 hours** of exposure — the sooner the better (ideally within 24 hours)
- Full course: **28 days** of antiretroviral drugs
- PEP is NOT a substitute for prevention (condoms, PrEP)

## When to Offer PEP

### Eligible Exposures (offer PEP if source is HIV-positive or unknown status)
- **Sexual exposure:** unprotected sex, condom breakage, sexual assault/rape
- **Occupational:** needlestick injury, mucous membrane splash with blood/body fluids
- **Other:** sharing injection equipment, bite with blood exposure

### Do NOT Offer PEP
- Exposure was >72 hours ago
- Exposed person is already HIV-positive
- Source is confirmed HIV-negative (with recent test)
- Negligible risk exposure (intact skin contact with blood)

## Recommended Regimen (WHO 2024)

### Preferred: TLD (fixed-dose combination)
- **Tenofovir (TDF) 300 mg** + **Lamivudine (3TC) 300 mg** + **Dolutegravir (DTG) 50 mg**
- One tablet once daily for 28 days
- TLD is preferred: lower pill burden, fewer side effects, better completion rates

### Alternative (if TLD unavailable)
- TDF 300 mg + 3TC 300 mg (or FTC 200 mg) + LPV/r 400/100 mg twice daily
- Or: TDF/3TC + EFV 600 mg once daily (avoid in first trimester pregnancy)

### Children (weight-based dosing)
- Same drug classes, adjusted by weight
- **REFER to HIV clinician** for pediatric PEP dosing

## Before Starting PEP — Mandatory Assessment
1. **HIV test** — rapid test BEFORE starting PEP (do not give PEP if already positive)
2. **Hepatitis B status** — TDF covers Hep B; stopping TDF can cause Hep B flare
3. **Pregnancy test** — TLD is safe in pregnancy (DTG safe in all trimesters per WHO 2024)
4. **Renal function** — TDF can affect kidneys; ask about kidney disease
5. **Drug interactions** — ask about current medications
6. **Time since exposure** — must be within 72 hours

## Adherence Support
- Explain importance of completing all 28 days
- Common side effects: nausea (first few days), headache, fatigue — usually mild
- Give antiemetic (metoclopramide) if nausea is severe
- Schedule follow-up at 1 week to check adherence and side effects
- Completion rates improve with counseling and fewer pills (TLD = 1 pill/day)

## Follow-Up Testing
- HIV test at **1 month** after completing PEP
- HIV test at **3 months** after exposure (definitive result)
- Hepatitis B and C test at 3 months if source status unknown
- STI screening if sexual exposure

## Sexual Assault / Rape
- PEP is a PRIORITY — do not delay for other assessments
- Also provide: emergency contraception, STI treatment, hepatitis B vaccine
- Psychological support and counseling
- Document injuries (with consent)
- **REFER** to appropriate facility for comprehensive care

## Danger Signs During PEP — Refer
- Severe rash or allergic reaction
- Jaundice (yellow eyes/skin) — possible liver toxicity
- Severe abdominal pain
- Signs of acute HIV seroconversion (fever, rash, lymphadenopathy 2-4 weeks after exposure)

Source: WHO Guidelines for HIV Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (2024); CDC MMWR Antiretroviral Postexposure Prophylaxis (2025); WHO Consolidated Guidelines on HIV Prevention (2024)
