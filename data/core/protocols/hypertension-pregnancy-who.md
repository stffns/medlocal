# WHO: Hypertension in Pregnancy for Community Health Workers

**Classification.**
**Chronic Hypertension:**
- BP ≥140/90 BEFORE pregnancy or before 20 weeks
- Continue pre-pregnancy medication (but switch if on ACE inhibitors/ARBs — teratogenic)
- Safe drugs in pregnancy: Methyldopa, Nifedipine, Labetalol

**Gestational Hypertension:**
- BP ≥140/90 AFTER 20 weeks, no proteinuria
- Monitor weekly, check for pre-eclampsia progression
- Start medication if BP ≥140/90 persists

**Pre-eclampsia — see dedicated pre-eclampsia protocol:**
- BP ≥140/90 + proteinuria after 20 weeks
- Severe if BP ≥160/110, headache, visual changes, epigastric pain

**Treatment in Pregnancy.**
**First-Line: Methyldopa:**
- 250 mg 2-3 times daily, increase up to 500 mg 3 times daily
- Safest antihypertensive in pregnancy — most evidence
- Side effects: drowsiness, dry mouth

**Second-Line: Nifedipine (slow-release):**
- 20 mg twice daily, increase up to 40 mg twice daily
- Do NOT use sublingual nifedipine (rapid BP drop dangerous for fetus)

**Third-Line: Labetalol:**
- 100 mg twice daily, increase up to 200 mg 3 times daily
- Avoid in asthma

**AVOID in Pregnancy:**
- **ACE inhibitors** (enalapril, lisinopril) — fetal kidney damage, death
- **ARBs** (losartan, valsartan) — same risk as ACE inhibitors
- **Atenolol** — fetal growth restriction
- **Diuretics** — reduce placental blood flow (unless heart failure)

**Monitoring.**
- Check BP at EVERY antenatal visit
- If BP ≥140/90: check urine protein (dipstick) at every visit
- Target: BP <140/90 (not too low — <110/70 reduces placental flow)
- Check fetal growth at each visit (hypertension causes growth restriction)

**Acute Severe Hypertension in Pregnancy (BP ≥160/110) — EMERGENCY.**
1. **REFER URGENTLY**
2. **Nifedipine** 10 mg oral (bite and swallow), repeat in 30 min if still ≥160/110
3. OR **Labetalol** 200 mg oral
4. OR **Hydralazine** 5 mg IV (at facility)
5. Monitor BP every 15 minutes
6. Check for pre-eclampsia signs: headache, vision, epigastric pain
7. If convulsions: see eclampsia protocol — give magnesium sulfate

**After Delivery.**
- BP may worsen in first 3-5 days postpartum
- Continue medication, adjust as needed
- Switch to any safe antihypertensive (ACE inhibitors safe postpartum, even while breastfeeding)
- Monitor BP daily for first week, then weekly
- Most gestational hypertension resolves within 6-12 weeks
- If BP still elevated at 12 weeks postpartum: diagnose chronic hypertension

**Danger Signs — Refer Immediately.**
- BP ≥160/110 at any time
- Headache not responding to paracetamol
- Visual disturbances (blurred, spots, flashing)
- Upper abdominal pain
- Sudden swelling of face/hands
- Decreased fetal movement
- Convulsions (eclampsia)
- Vaginal bleeding with hypertension (placental abruption)

Source: WHO Recommendations for Prevention and Treatment of Pre-eclampsia and Eclampsia; WHO Recommendations on Drug Treatment for Non-severe Hypertension in Pregnancy (2022)
