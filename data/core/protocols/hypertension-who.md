# WHO: Hypertension Management for Community Health Workers

**Diagnosis.**
- Blood pressure ≥140/90 mmHg on two separate occasions at least 1 day apart
- Pre-hypertension: 120-139/80-89 — lifestyle advice, recheck in 1 year

**Classification.**
- Grade 1: 140-159/90-99 — lifestyle + medication if risk factors
- Grade 2: 160-179/100-109 — lifestyle + medication
- Grade 3: ≥180/≥110 — **URGENT** — start treatment immediately, refer

**Treatment.**
**Lifestyle (all patients):**
- Reduce salt: <5g/day (less than 1 teaspoon)
- Exercise: 30 minutes moderate activity most days
- Healthy diet: fruits, vegetables, reduce fatty/fried foods
- Limit alcohol, stop smoking
- Reduce weight if overweight

**First-Line Medication:**
- **Amlodipine** 5 mg once daily (increase to 10 mg if needed)
- OR **Enalapril** 5 mg once daily (increase to 20 mg)
- If not controlled: combine amlodipine + enalapril
- Add **Hydrochlorothiazide** 12.5-25 mg if still not controlled

**Target:**
- Blood pressure <140/90 mmHg
- In diabetes or kidney disease: <130/80 mmHg

**Monitoring.**
- Check BP monthly until controlled
- Once stable: every 3 months
- Check kidney function and potassium annually if on enalapril

**Danger Signs — Refer Immediately.**
- BP ≥180/110 with headache, chest pain, or visual changes (hypertensive crisis)
- Sudden weakness on one side of body (stroke)
- Severe chest pain (heart attack)
- Sudden difficulty breathing
- Blood in urine with high BP

Source: WHO HEARTS Technical Package (2018); WHO PEN for Primary Care (2020, updated 2023)
