# WHO: Home Remedies and Foods Contraindicated in Infants

**Scope.** Community health workers must actively counsel against common folk remedies given to infants under 12 months. Many traditional practices that are safe in older children and adults cause serious harm or death in young infants. When a caregiver asks "can I give X to my baby for Y", check this list BEFORE answering.

**Honey — Contraindicated Under 12 Months.**
- **Do NOT give honey in any form to any child under 12 months**, not even a small taste, not even on a pacifier, not even mixed in tea or milk, not even for cough.
- **Why:** Honey commonly contains *Clostridium botulinum* spores. Adult guts neutralize them, but infant guts allow germination — the toxin causes **infant botulism**: descending flaccid paralysis, weak cry, poor feeding, constipation, respiratory failure, death if untreated.
- **For cough in infants:** honey is NOT an option. Safe measures are listed below.

**Plain Water — Contraindicated Under 6 Months.**
- Exclusively breastfed or formula-fed babies under 6 months do **NOT need water**, even in hot climates — breastmilk is >85% water and adjusts to temperature.
- **Why:** Giving plain water causes **water intoxication / hyponatremia** — seizures, hypothermia, brain swelling. Infant kidneys cannot dilute a water load.
- If a caregiver is worried about hydration, the answer is **more frequent breastfeeding**, not water.

**Cow's Milk — Contraindicated Under 12 Months.**
- Whole cow's milk as a drink is **not safe before 12 months**.
- **Why:** iron deficiency, microscopic gut bleeding, high renal solute load, allergy. Use breastmilk or infant formula only.

**Herbal Teas and Infusions — Avoid in Infants.**
- Chamomile, anise (anís), fennel, "agüitas" and similar infusions given for colic, gas, or sleep are **not recommended under 12 months**.
- **Why:** they displace breastmilk (hyponatremia risk), may be contaminated, and some (star anise) have caused neurotoxicity and seizures in case reports. If colic is the concern, the answer is positioning, burping, and reassurance — not tea.

**Over-the-Counter Cough and Cold Medicines — Contraindicated Under 2 Years.**
- Antitussives (dextromethorphan), decongestants (pseudoephedrine, phenylephrine), antihistamines (diphenhydramine), combination cold syrups: **do NOT give to children under 2 years**, and avoid under 6 years when possible.
- **Why:** the FDA and AAP link these to serious harm and deaths from overdose and cardiovascular events in young children. No proven benefit.

**Home-made Oral Rehydration — Use Only WHO ORS.**
- "Suero casero" made with salt and sugar at unmeasured amounts has wrong osmolarity and can **worsen dehydration or cause hypernatremia**. Use packet ORS. If no ORS is available, the WHO interim formula is 1 level teaspoon salt + 8 level teaspoons sugar in 1 liter of clean water — and transport to a facility.

**Safe Cough Management in Young Infants.**
1. Any infant **under 3 months** with a cough needs **URGENT REFERRAL** — possible pneumonia, pertussis, RSV bronchiolitis.
2. For mild cough in infants ≥3 months without danger signs: saline nasal drops + bulb syringe for congestion, frequent breastfeeding, semi-upright positioning, humidified air.
3. Danger signs requiring immediate referral: fast breathing, chest indrawing, grunting, cyanosis, unable to feed, lethargy, fever ≥38 °C in <3 months or hypothermia <35.5 °C, apnea, seizures.
4. **Never:** honey under 12 months, cough syrups under 2 years, aspirin in any child under 12.

**Always Ask Before Giving Any Remedy to an Infant.**
1. How old exactly is the baby (months, not "small")?
2. Is the baby exclusively breastfed?
3. What has the caregiver already given today?
4. Are there any danger signs?

Source: WHO/UNICEF IMCI Guidelines; WHO Infant and Young Child Feeding (2021); WHO Pocket Book of Hospital Care for Children; AAP clinical reports on cough and cold medications; FDA advisory on pediatric OTC cough and cold products.
