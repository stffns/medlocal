# WHO: Malaria in Pregnancy

## Why Pregnancy Is High Risk
- Pregnant women are more susceptible to malaria
- Malaria in pregnancy causes: severe anemia, low birth weight, preterm delivery, stillbirth, maternal death
- Placental malaria can occur even without symptoms

## Prevention — THREE Pillars

### 1. Insecticide-Treated Bed Nets (ITNs)
- EVERY pregnant woman should sleep under an ITN every night
- Provide at first antenatal visit
- Also protects the newborn after birth

### 2. Intermittent Preventive Treatment (IPTp)
- **Sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine (SP):** 3 tablets (1500/75 mg) as single dose
- Give at EACH antenatal visit from 2nd trimester (≥13 weeks)
- At least 1 month apart between doses
- Minimum 3 doses during pregnancy
- Give under direct observation (DOT)
- Can give on empty stomach
- Do NOT give in first trimester
- Do NOT give to HIV+ women on cotrimoxazole (use cotrimoxazole instead)

### 3. Prompt Diagnosis and Treatment
- Test with RDT at every antenatal visit in endemic areas
- Test ANY pregnant woman with fever

## Treatment of Malaria in Pregnancy

### First Trimester
- **Quinine** 10 mg/kg every 8 hours for 7 days
- PLUS **Clindamycin** 5 mg/kg every 8 hours for 7 days
- ACTs NOT recommended in first trimester (limited safety data)

### Second and Third Trimester
- **ACT (Artemether-lumefantrine)** — standard dosing for 3 days
- Same as non-pregnant adults (see malaria protocol)
- Safe and effective in 2nd/3rd trimester

### Severe Malaria in Pregnancy — EMERGENCY
- **REFER URGENTLY** to hospital
- **IV Artesunate** 2.4 mg/kg at 0, 12, 24 hours, then daily — same as non-pregnant
- Severe malaria in pregnancy has very high mortality
- Monitor blood glucose (hypoglycemia more common in pregnancy)
- Fetal monitoring — risk of fetal distress

## Anemia in Malaria + Pregnancy
- Malaria worsens anemia — check hemoglobin
- If Hb <7 g/dL: **REFER** for possible transfusion
- Iron + folic acid supplementation continues throughout pregnancy
- Treat malaria FIRST, then address anemia

## After Delivery
- Test newborn for malaria if mother had malaria during pregnancy
- Continue bed net use for mother and newborn
- Breastfeeding is safe — antimalarials pass in very small amounts

## Danger Signs — Refer Immediately
- High fever in pregnancy (any trimester)
- Severe anemia (very pale, breathless)
- Unable to eat or drink
- Confusion or altered consciousness
- Convulsions (could be cerebral malaria OR eclampsia — both emergencies)
- Decreased fetal movement

Source: WHO Malaria Treatment Guidelines 3rd Edition; WHO Recommendations on Antenatal Care (2016); WHO IPTp Policy Brief
