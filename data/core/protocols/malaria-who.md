# WHO Malaria Treatment Guidelines (Summary)

**Uncomplicated P. falciparum Malaria.**
**First-line Treatment:**
Artemisinin-based Combination Therapy (ACT) for 3 days.

**Dosing by weight band — read carefully.**
Artemether-lumefantrine: 6-dose regimen over 3 days. Each dose contains artemether 20mg + lumefantrine 120mg. The correct weight band is determined by the child's weight in kilograms; a child whose weight falls between two bands belongs to the LOWER band (the upper number of each band is inclusive).

- **5 to 14 kg (inclusive) → 1 tablet per dose.** Examples: a 6 kg infant, a 10 kg toddler, a 12 kg child, a 14 kg child all receive **1 tablet** per dose. Do NOT give 2 tablets to a child ≤14 kg.
- **15 to 24 kg (inclusive) → 2 tablets per dose.** Examples: a 15 kg, 18 kg, 20 kg, or 24 kg child receives 2 tablets per dose.
- **25 to 34 kg (inclusive) → 3 tablets per dose.** Examples: 26 kg, 30 kg, 34 kg.
- **35 kg and above → 4 tablets per dose** (adult dose). Examples: 40 kg adolescent, 60 kg adult.

Give at 0, 8, 24, 36, 48, and 60 hours — that is **6 doses total over 3 days**. Give with food or milk (fatty food improves lumefantrine absorption).

**Alternative ACTs:**
- Artesunate + amodiaquine
- Artesunate + mefloquine
- Dihydroartemisinin + piperaquine

**Severe Malaria — EMERGENCY.**
**Immediate Actions:**
1. **REFER URGENTLY** to hospital
2. Pre-referral treatment:
   - Artesunate rectal (if injection not available):
     - Under 1 year: DO NOT USE rectal artesunate, refer immediately
     - 1-5 years (≤20 kg): one 100mg suppository
   - OR Artesunate IM: 2.4 mg/kg

**At Hospital:**
- IV artesunate: 2.4 mg/kg at 0, 12, and 24 hours, then once daily
- Minimum 24 hours of IV artesunate before switching to oral ACT
- Monitor blood glucose (hypoglycemia is common)

**Malaria in Pregnancy.**
- First trimester: quinine + clindamycin for 7 days
- Second/third trimester: ACT (artemether-lumefantrine recommended)
- Severe malaria any trimester: IV artesunate (same as non-pregnant)

**Danger Signs — Refer Immediately.**
- Unable to sit or stand
- Unable to eat or drink
- Convulsions
- Persistent vomiting
- Lethargy or unconsciousness
- Dark/brown urine (blackwater fever)
- Severe anemia (very pale palms)
- Jaundice (yellow eyes)

**Prevention.**
- Insecticide-treated bed nets (ITNs) — sleep under net every night
- Intermittent preventive treatment in pregnancy (IPTp):
  - Sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine at each antenatal visit
  - Starting from second trimester
  - At least 1 month apart, minimum 3 doses

**Diagnosis.**
- Rapid Diagnostic Test (RDT) or microscopy before treatment
- If RDT not available in high-transmission area: treat based on clinical suspicion in children under 5 with fever

Source: WHO Guidelines for Malaria Treatment, 3rd Edition (2015, updated 2022)
