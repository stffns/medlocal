# WHO: Malnutrition in Adults and Adolescents

## Assessment — BMI and MUAC

### BMI (Body Mass Index) = weight (kg) / height (m)²
- <16.0: Severe malnutrition
- 16.0-16.9: Moderate malnutrition
- 17.0-18.4: Mild malnutrition
- 18.5-24.9: Normal
- ≥25.0: Overweight

### MUAC (Mid-Upper Arm Circumference) — Quick Field Assessment
- <19 cm: Severe malnutrition
- 19-21.9 cm: Moderate malnutrition
- ≥22 cm: Normal
- Measure at midpoint of left upper arm

## When to Suspect Malnutrition
- Visible wasting (thin limbs, prominent ribs, loose skin)
- Recent unintentional weight loss
- Chronic illness (HIV, TB, cancer)
- Elderly with poor appetite
- Bilateral pitting edema (kwashiorkor — protein deficiency)
- Fatigue, weakness, frequent infections

## Management

### Severe Malnutrition WITH Complications — REFER
Complications: unable to eat, dehydration, severe infection, hypothermia, hypoglycemia, severe edema
- Stabilize before referral:
  - If hypoglycemic: give sugar water (50 mL of 10% glucose)
  - Keep warm
  - Start feeding small frequent amounts if conscious

### Severe Malnutrition WITHOUT Complications
- Outpatient therapeutic feeding
- RUTF (Ready-to-Use Therapeutic Food) if available:
  - 175-200 kcal/kg/day
  - Divided into 4-6 meals
- If no RUTF: high-calorie diet with protein (eggs, milk, peanuts, beans + oil)
- Supplement: multivitamin, folic acid
- Treat underlying cause (TB, HIV, depression)
- Weekly follow-up with weight monitoring

### Moderate Malnutrition
- Nutrition counseling:
  - Eat 4-5 meals per day
  - Add oil/fat to food for extra calories
  - Include protein at every meal
  - Diverse diet: grains + legumes + vegetables + animal-source protein
- Supplementary food if available (fortified blended flour)
- Treat infections, deworm
- Follow up every 2 weeks

### Malnutrition in HIV/TB Patients
- Start nutritional support BEFORE or WITH treatment
- Increased calorie needs: +10% if asymptomatic, +20-30% if symptomatic
- ART/TB drugs may improve appetite — continue monitoring

## Refeeding Syndrome — DANGER
When severely malnourished patient starts eating again:
- Risk of heart failure, seizures, death
- Signs: rapid pulse, swelling, confusion in first week of feeding
- Prevent by: starting feeds slowly, small frequent meals, increasing gradually
- Monitor closely in first 7 days

## Danger Signs — Refer
- BMI <16 or MUAC <19 cm with any complication
- Severe bilateral edema
- Unable to eat or drink
- Hypothermia (<35°C)
- Severe infection (fever, cough, diarrhea)
- Altered consciousness

Source: WHO Guidelines for the Inpatient Treatment of Severely Malnourished Children (adapted for adults); WHO IMAI Acute Care
