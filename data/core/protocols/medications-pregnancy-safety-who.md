# WHO: Medication Safety in Pregnancy

**Scope.** Community health workers must screen every medication given to a pregnant woman for fetal and maternal safety. Many common over-the-counter and first-line drugs are contraindicated in pregnancy, especially in the third trimester. When in doubt, **do NOT give the drug and refer to antenatal care**.

**Contraindicated — Any Trimester.**
- **Warfarin** (fetal bleeding, embryopathy) — use low-molecular-weight heparin if anticoagulation needed
- **ACE inhibitors** (enalapril, lisinopril, captopril) and **ARBs** (losartan, valsartan) — fetal kidney damage, oligohydramnios
- **Misoprostol** for any reason outside obstetric indication — can cause abortion, uterine rupture
- **Statins** (atorvastatin, simvastatin) — stop before conception
- **Retinoids** (isotretinoin, high-dose vitamin A >10,000 IU/day) — severe malformations
- **Methotrexate**, chemotherapy agents — teratogenic
- **Tetracyclines** (doxycycline, tetracycline) after week 15 — fetal teeth, bone
- **Fluoroquinolones** (ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin) — cartilage concerns, avoid when alternatives exist

**Contraindicated — Third Trimester (after week 28-30).**
- **Aspirin at analgesic or cardiac dose (≥300 mg)** — premature closure of fetal ductus arteriosus, maternal and neonatal bleeding at delivery, delayed labor. This is DIFFERENT from the low-dose 75 mg/day preventive aspirin used from week 12 in women at high risk of pre-eclampsia; the preventive dose is safe, the analgesic dose is NOT.
- **All NSAIDs** — ibuprofen, diclofenac, naproxen, indomethacin — same fetal ductus and oligohydramnios risk
- **Codeine and opioids near delivery** — neonatal respiratory depression and withdrawal

**Safe With Conditions (requires trimester check).**
- **Metronidazole** — ALWAYS state the trimester rule when recommending this drug in pregnancy:
  - **1st trimester (weeks 1-12): AVOID.** Defer metronidazole until 2nd trimester when possible. For vaginal symptoms in 1st trimester, use Clotrimazole pessary topically instead. If trichomoniasis is confirmed and symptomatic, 2g single dose is acceptable per CDC but discuss with clinician first.
  - **2nd and 3rd trimester: SAFE.** Dose for BV: 500 mg twice daily for 7 days (NOT the 2g single dose — 7-day course has lower recurrence). For trichomoniasis: 2g single dose is acceptable.
  - Not teratogenic based on >5,000 exposed pregnancies (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2364581/), but the 1st trimester caution is standard practice.

**Safer Alternatives.**
- **Pain and fever:** Paracetamol (acetaminophen) is first-line throughout pregnancy at standard doses
- **Hypertension in pregnancy:** Methyldopa, labetalol, nifedipine (see pre-eclampsia protocol)
- **Infection:** Amoxicillin, cephalexin, azithromycin, erythromycin (not estolate) are generally safe

**Red-Flag Presentation.**
A pregnant woman (especially ≥20 weeks) with severe headache, visual changes, upper abdominal pain, or swelling who has taken aspirin, ibuprofen, or any NSAID needs **URGENT REFERRAL** — the symptoms may be pre-eclampsia, and the NSAID increases bleeding risk on top of an already dangerous condition.

**Always Ask Before Giving Any Drug.**
1. Is she pregnant or could she be pregnant?
2. Which trimester?
3. What has she already taken today?
4. Is this drug necessary, or can it wait until a clinician reviews her?

Source: WHO Model List of Essential Medicines (2023); WHO Pharmacological Treatment of Hypertensive Disorders in Pregnancy (2018); WHO Guidelines for the Management of Common Illnesses in Pregnancy.
