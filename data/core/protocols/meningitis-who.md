# WHO: Meningitis for Community Health Workers

## When to Suspect Meningitis — EMERGENCY
Meningitis can kill within hours. Suspect if:
- Severe headache + fever + stiff neck (cannot touch chin to chest)
- In infants: bulging fontanelle, fever, irritability, poor feeding, high-pitched cry
- Altered consciousness, confusion, drowsiness
- Sensitivity to light
- Petechial rash (small purple/red spots that do NOT fade when pressed)

**ANY suspected meningitis = REFER URGENTLY to hospital**

## Pre-Referral Treatment
If transfer will take >30 minutes, give first dose of antibiotic:
- **Ceftriaxone** IM:
  - Children: 50-100 mg/kg (max 2g) single dose
  - Adults: 2g single dose
- If ceftriaxone not available: **Ampicillin** 50 mg/kg IM
- Give paracetamol for fever
- Start IV fluids if available

## Epidemic Meningitis (meningococcal)
- Occurs in outbreaks, especially in the "meningitis belt" (sub-Saharan Africa)
- Highly contagious — droplet spread
- Vaccination: meningococcal conjugate vaccine (MenACWY)
- During outbreak: treat all suspected cases immediately, do not wait for lab confirmation
- Close contacts: Ciprofloxacin 500 mg single dose (adults) or Ceftriaxone IM (children)

## TB Meningitis
- Slower onset (days to weeks): headache, fever, confusion, weight loss
- History of TB or TB contact
- **REFER** — needs prolonged anti-TB treatment + steroids

## Neonatal Meningitis
- Fever or hypothermia + irritability or lethargy + poor feeding
- Bulging fontanelle (if present = strong indicator)
- **REFER URGENTLY** — high mortality without treatment
- Pre-referral: Ampicillin 50 mg/kg IM + Gentamicin 5 mg/kg IM

## Prevention
- Vaccination: routine childhood schedule includes Hib and PCV (prevent common causes)
- Meningococcal vaccine in endemic areas
- Prompt treatment of ear infections and sinusitis (can spread to brain)

Source: WHO Meningitis Guidelines (2023); WHO Managing Meningitis Epidemics in Africa; WHO IMCI
