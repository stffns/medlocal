# WHO: Meningococcemia (Meningococcal Septicemia) — EMERGENCY

**What Is Meningococcemia?.**
- Bloodstream infection by Neisseria meningitidis (meningococcus)
- Can occur WITH or WITHOUT meningitis
- **Fulminant form can kill in hours** — fastest lethal bacterial infection
- Most common in children <5 years and adolescents 15-19 years
- Transmission: respiratory droplets, close contact

**When to Suspect — REFER IMMEDIATELY.**
Suspect meningococcemia if fever PLUS any of:
- **Petechial rash** — small purple/red spots (1-2 mm) that do NOT fade when pressed (glass test)
- **Purpura** — larger purple patches, may spread rapidly before your eyes
- Rash that starts on trunk/legs and spreads within minutes to hours
- Cold hands and feet with fever (peripheral shutdown)
- Rapid deterioration: alert → confused → unconscious in hours
- Leg pain or refusal to walk (in children)
- Mottled or grey skin color

**The Glass Test (Tumbler Test).**
1. Press a clear glass firmly against the rash
2. If spots do NOT fade under pressure → **suspect meningococcemia**
3. If spots fade (blanch) → less likely, but still monitor closely
4. A negative glass test does NOT rule out meningococcemia in early stages
5. **If in doubt, treat as meningococcemia — the cost of delay is death**

**Immediate Actions — Do NOT Wait.**
1. **Give antibiotics IMMEDIATELY** — before transport, before blood tests, before anything
   - **Ceftriaxone** IM: Adults 2g, Children 50-100 mg/kg (max 2g) — single dose
   - If no ceftriaxone: **Benzylpenicillin** IV/IM: Adults 2.4g, Children 50 mg/kg
   - If no injectable antibiotics: give **oral amoxicillin** while arranging urgent transfer
2. **REFER URGENTLY** — this patient needs ICU-level care
3. **IV fluids** if available: Normal Saline 20 mL/kg bolus, repeat if still shocked
4. Keep warm — patients lose heat rapidly due to poor circulation
5. Position: lie flat (shock position) — do NOT elevate head
6. Monitor breathing — may need airway support
7. **Do NOT wait** for lab results, lumbar puncture, or blood cultures before treating

**Progression Warning Signs.**
Meningococcemia can progress through these stages in hours:
- **Stage 1:** Fever + non-specific symptoms (looks like flu)
- **Stage 2:** Petechial rash appears + cold extremities + tachycardia
- **Stage 3:** Purpura spreading + hypotension + confusion
- **Stage 4:** Purpura fulminans (large areas of skin necrosis) + multi-organ failure + DIC
- **Early recognition at Stage 1-2 saves lives**

**Close Contacts — Prophylaxis.**
Give prophylaxis to household members and close contacts within 24 hours:
- **Adults:** Ciprofloxacin 500 mg oral single dose (preferred)
- **Children:** Ceftriaxone 125 mg IM single dose (<12 years) or 250 mg IM (≥12 years)
- **Pregnant women:** Ceftriaxone 250 mg IM single dose (ciprofloxacin contraindicated)
- Close contact = same household, shared sleeping, kissing, shared utensils in past 7 days

**What NOT to Do.**
- Do NOT delay antibiotics for any reason (lumbar puncture, blood tests, transport)
- Do NOT perform lumbar puncture if suspected meningococcemia (risk of herniation + delays treatment)
- Do NOT give aspirin or ibuprofen (bleeding risk with DIC)
- Do NOT dismiss petechial rash with fever as "viral" — always do the glass test

**Danger Signs — Refer Immediately.**
- Any non-blanching rash with fever
- Rapidly spreading purpura
- Cold extremities with fever
- Altered consciousness with fever
- Shock signs: weak rapid pulse, low BP, mottled skin
