# WHO: Essential Newborn Care

**Immediate Care at Birth (first 60 minutes).**
**Normal Birth — Steps in Order:**
1. Dry the baby thoroughly with clean cloth
2. Assess breathing — is baby crying or breathing well?
3. Clamp and cut the cord (after 1-3 minutes, not immediately)
4. Place baby skin-to-skin on mother's chest
5. Cover baby and mother together (keep warm)
6. Initiate breastfeeding within first hour

**Baby NOT Breathing at Birth:**
1. Clamp and cut cord immediately
2. Move to warm, flat surface
3. Clear airway — position head in neutral position, suction mouth then nose if needed
4. Stimulate — rub back firmly 2-3 times
5. If still not breathing: begin ventilation with bag and mask
   - Rate: 40-60 breaths per minute
   - Watch for chest rise
6. If no improvement after 1 minute of ventilation: **REFER URGENTLY**

**Thermal Protection (keeping baby warm).**
- Room temperature ≥25°C
- Skin-to-skin contact with mother
- Cover with blanket, including head (cap)
- Delay bathing for at least 24 hours
- Do NOT separate baby from mother

**Hypothermia Signs:**
- Cold to touch (feet, hands, body)
- Temperature <36.5°C
- Action: Rewarm with skin-to-skin, cover with warm blankets
- If temperature <35.5°C: **REFER URGENTLY** — severe hypothermia

**Breastfeeding.**
- Initiate within first hour of birth
- Exclusive breastfeeding — no water, no formula, no other foods for 6 months
- Feed on demand — at least 8-12 times per 24 hours
- Good attachment: mouth wide open, lower lip turned out, chin touching breast

**Breastfeeding Problems:**
- Not attaching: reposition, ensure mouth wide open
- Not enough milk: most common reason is infrequent feeding — feed more often
- Sore/cracked nipples: check attachment, apply breastmilk to nipples

**Cord Care.**
- Keep cord stump clean and dry
- Apply chlorhexidine 7.1% gel/solution to stump daily for first week (in areas with high neonatal mortality)
- Do NOT apply traditional substances (ash, cow dung, etc.)
- Fold diaper below cord stump

**Signs of Cord Infection — Refer:**
- Redness extending to skin around cord
- Pus or discharge from cord
- Bad smell from cord
- Baby has fever

**Danger Signs in Newborns — Refer Immediately.**
- Not feeding or unable to breastfeed
- Convulsions
- Fast breathing (≥60/min) or severe chest indrawing
- Fever (≥38°C) or hypothermia (<35.5°C)
- Severe jaundice (yellow skin in first 24 hours, or extending to palms/soles)
- Umbilical infection (redness, pus, bad smell)
- Lethargy or reduced movement
- Skin pustules or blisters

**Eye Care.**
- Clean eyes with clean water if discharge
- Apply tetracycline 1% eye ointment to both eyes within first hour
- Prevents ophthalmia neonatorum (gonorrhea/chlamydia infection)

**Immunizations at Birth.**
- BCG (tuberculosis)
- OPV-0 (oral polio vaccine — zero dose)
- Hepatitis B — first dose within 24 hours

**Weight Assessment.**
- Normal birth weight: 2500-4000g
- Low birth weight: <2500g — needs extra warmth, feeding support, close monitoring
- Very low birth weight: <1500g — **REFER** to facility with neonatal care

Source: WHO Recommendations on Newborn Health (2017, updated 2022)
