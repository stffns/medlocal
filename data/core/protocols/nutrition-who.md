# WHO: Malnutrition Assessment and Management for Community Health Workers

**Assessment — MUAC (Mid-Upper Arm Circumference).**
Measure MUAC on left arm, midpoint between shoulder and elbow.

**Classification (children 6-59 months):**
- Green (≥12.5 cm): Normal nutrition
- Yellow (11.5-12.4 cm): Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM)
- Red (<11.5 cm): Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM)

**Edema Check:**
Press both thumbs on top of both feet for 3 seconds.
- If pitting remains after release = bilateral pitting edema
- Any child with bilateral pitting edema = SAM regardless of MUAC

**Management.**
**Normal Nutrition (MUAC ≥12.5 cm):**
1. Counsel on continued breastfeeding (up to 2 years)
2. Diverse diet: grains, legumes, vegetables, fruits, animal-source foods
3. Rescreen at next community visit

**Moderate Acute Malnutrition — MAM (MUAC 11.5-12.4 cm):**
1. Refer to supplementary feeding program if available
2. Provide nutrition counseling:
   - Feed 5 times per day (3 meals + 2 snacks)
   - Add oil or fat to food (increases calories)
   - Give diverse foods — especially eggs, milk, legumes, green vegetables
3. Give micronutrient supplements (iron, vitamin A, zinc)
4. Treat any infections (deworming, malaria if present)
5. Follow up in 2 weeks — measure MUAC again

**Severe Acute Malnutrition — SAM:**
**SAM WITHOUT Complications (child alert, appetite present):**
1. Outpatient treatment with RUTF (Ready-to-Use Therapeutic Food)
2. RUTF dosing by weight:
   - 3.5-4.9 kg: 100g/day (1 sachet)
   - 5.0-6.9 kg: 150g/day (1.5 sachets)
   - 7.0-8.4 kg: 200g/day (2 sachets)
   - 8.5-9.4 kg: 225g/day (2.5 sachets)
   - 9.5-10.4 kg: 250g/day (2.5 sachets)
   - 10.5-11.9 kg: 275g/day (3 sachets)
3. Continue breastfeeding
4. Routine medicines: amoxicillin for 7 days, deworming (if >12 months), vitamin A
5. Follow up weekly — measure MUAC and weight
6. Discharge when MUAC ≥12.5 cm for 2 consecutive visits and no edema

**SAM WITH Complications — REFER URGENTLY:**
Complications include:
- Bilateral pitting edema (+++ severe)
- Not able to eat/no appetite (fails appetite test)
- Fever (≥38.5°C) or hypothermia (<35°C)
- Dehydration
- Severe pneumonia
- Very weak or lethargic
- Severe anemia (very pale palms)
- Skin lesions (peeling, ulcers)

**Vitamin A Supplementation (routine).**
- 6-11 months: 100,000 IU every 6 months
- 12-59 months: 200,000 IU every 6 months

**Deworming.**
- 12-23 months: albendazole 200 mg or mebendazole 250 mg, single dose
- 24-59 months: albendazole 400 mg or mebendazole 500 mg, single dose
- Repeat every 6 months in endemic areas

**Danger Signs — Refer Immediately.**
- MUAC <11.5 cm with any complication
- Bilateral pitting edema
- Visible severe wasting (very thin, loose skin)
- Not able to eat or drink
- Persistent vomiting
- High fever or hypothermia

Source: WHO Guidelines for the Management of Acute Malnutrition (2013, updated 2023); WHO Community-based Management of SAM
