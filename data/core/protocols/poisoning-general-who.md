# WHO: Poisoning and Toxic Exposure — General Approach

For pesticide / cholinergic emergencies see `poisoning-organophosphate-who.md`.

## General Approach — ALL Poisonings
1. **Ensure your own safety first** — ventilate area if toxic fumes, wear
   gloves for chemical contact
2. **Remove the source:** remove contaminated clothing, wash skin with soap
   and water for at least 15 minutes if chemical contact
3. **Identify the poison** — keep the container, note the name
4. **Note** time of exposure, amount, route (swallowed, inhaled, skin)
5. **REFER URGENTLY** to hospital

## Swallowed Poisons

### Do NOT induce vomiting if:
- Corrosive (acid, bleach, battery fluid) — burns twice
- Petroleum product (kerosene, gasoline) — aspiration pneumonia risk
- Patient is unconscious or having convulsions
- Sharp objects swallowed

### If conscious and poison is NOT corrosive/petroleum:
- **Activated charcoal** (within 1 hour of ingestion):
  - Adults: 50 g in water
  - Children: 1 g/kg in water
- Do NOT give charcoal with corrosives, metals (iron, lithium), or alcohol

### Corrosive Ingestion (acid, bleach, caustic soda)
- Give small sips of water or milk to dilute (max 200 mL)
- Do NOT induce vomiting
- **REFER URGENTLY** — risk of esophageal perforation

## Paracetamol Overdose
- Dangerous if >150 mg/kg (children) or >10 g (adults) in a single dose
- Symptoms delayed 24–72 hours — patient may feel fine, then liver failure
- **REFER** for N-acetylcysteine within 8 hours if possible

## Inhaled Poisons (smoke, CO, chemicals)
1. Move patient to fresh air immediately
2. If not breathing: rescue breathing / CPR
3. Give oxygen if available
4. **REFER URGENTLY**

## Prevention
- Store chemicals and medicines out of children's reach, in original labeled
  containers; never in food or drink containers
- Use protective equipment when handling pesticides
- Ventilate when using chemicals indoors

Source: WHO/IPCS INTOX Guidelines; WHO Basic Emergency Care.
