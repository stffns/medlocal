# WHO: Organophosphate / Pesticide Poisoning — EMERGENCY

Also known as: insecticide poisoning, pesticide exposure, plaguicida,
insecticida, fumigación, envenenamiento por pesticidas, intoxicación por
organofosforados, intoxicación colinérgica. Typical patient: a farmer,
agricultural worker, field sprayer, or someone who was fumigating crops
(spraying potatoes, rice, cotton, vegetables) or mixing pesticide concentrate
without protective equipment. Can also occur after accidental ingestion.

Cholinergic toxidrome (SLUDGE) — signs appear within minutes to hours of
exposure. This IS what "poisoning after spraying / fumigating crops" means.

**Recognise — Cholinergic / SLUDGE Signs:**
Pinpoint pupils (miosis, pupils like a pinhead); heavy sweating
(diaphoresis); excessive salivation, tears, runny nose; vomiting, diarrhea,
abdominal cramps; muscle twitching, weakness, fasciculations; difficulty
breathing, wheezing, chest tightness; blurred vision; confusion, agitation,
seizures, loss of consciousness; strong chemical, gasoline, or pesticide
smell on clothes, skin, breath.

**Step 1 — Protect yourself.** Wear gloves. Move patient to fresh air.

**Step 2 — Decontaminate the skin immediately.** Pesticide keeps absorbing
every minute it stays on the body.
- Remove ALL contaminated clothing (shoes, hat, watch included).
- Wash skin and hair with **soap and large amounts of water** for at least
  15 minutes. Skin folds, behind the ears, under nails.
- If eyes are exposed, irrigate with clean water for 15 minutes.
- Bag the contaminated clothing; do not let anyone wear it again.

**Step 3 — Atropine, as soon as available.**
- Adults: **1–2 mg IV or IM**, repeat every 5–10 minutes.
- Children: **0.02 mg/kg IV or IM**, repeat every 5–10 minutes.
- Endpoint: keep giving until secretions dry and lungs clear.

**Step 4 — Keep the airway open.** Suction secretions. Recovery position if
unconscious.

**Step 5 — REFER URGENTLY.** Send the pesticide container or label with the
patient if possible.

**Do NOT** give morphine, aminophylline, or succinylcholine. Do NOT induce
vomiting. Do NOT leave the patient in contaminated clothing during transport.

Source: WHO Clinical Management of Acute Pesticide Intoxication; WHO/IPCS INTOX.
