# WHO: Poisoning and Toxic Exposure for Community Health Workers

## General Approach — ALL Poisonings
1. **Ensure your own safety first** — ventilate area if toxic fumes
2. **Remove the source:** Remove contaminated clothing, wash skin if chemical contact
3. **Identify the poison** if possible — keep the container, note the name
4. **Call for help — REFER URGENTLY** to hospital
5. **Note:** time of exposure, amount, route (swallowed, inhaled, skin)

## Swallowed Poisons

### Do NOT induce vomiting if:
- Corrosive substance (acid, bleach, battery fluid) — burns twice
- Petroleum product (kerosene, gasoline) — aspiration pneumonia risk
- Patient is unconscious or having convulsions
- Sharp objects swallowed

### If conscious and poison is NOT corrosive/petroleum:
- **Activated charcoal** (if available and within 1 hour of ingestion):
  - Adults: 50g in water
  - Children: 1g/kg in water
- Do NOT give with corrosives, metals (iron, lithium), or alcohol

### Corrosive Ingestion (acid, bleach, caustic soda)
- Give small sips of water or milk to dilute (max 200 mL)
- Do NOT induce vomiting
- **REFER URGENTLY** — risk of esophageal perforation

## Pesticide/Organophosphate Poisoning — EMERGENCY
Common in farming communities. Signs appear within minutes to hours.
- Excessive salivation, sweating, tears
- Small pupils (pinpoint)
- Vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal cramps
- Difficulty breathing, wheezing
- Confusion, seizures

### Treatment
1. Remove contaminated clothing, wash skin with soap and water
2. **Atropine** 1-2 mg IV/IM every 5-10 minutes until secretions dry
3. Maintain airway — suction secretions
4. **REFER URGENTLY**
5. Do NOT give morphine, aminophylline, or succinylcholine

## Paracetamol Overdose
- Dangerous if >150 mg/kg in a single dose (children) or >10g in adults
- Symptoms delayed 24-72 hours — may feel fine initially, then liver failure
- **REFER** for N-acetylcysteine treatment within 8 hours if possible

## Inhaled Poisons (smoke, CO, chemicals)
1. Move patient to fresh air immediately
2. If not breathing: begin rescue breathing/CPR
3. Give oxygen if available
4. **REFER URGENTLY**

## Prevention
- Store chemicals and medicines out of children's reach, in original labeled containers
- Never store chemicals in food/drink containers
- Use protective equipment when handling pesticides
- Ventilate when using chemicals indoors
- Follow label instructions for pesticides

Source: WHO Clinical Management of Acute Pesticide Intoxication; WHO/IPCS INTOX Guidelines; WHO Basic Emergency Care
