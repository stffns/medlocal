# WHO: Pre-eclampsia and Eclampsia Management

**Definitions.**
**Hypertension in Pregnancy:**
- Blood pressure ≥140/90 mmHg on two readings at least 4 hours apart

**Pre-eclampsia:**
- Hypertension (≥140/90) PLUS proteinuria (≥1+ on dipstick)
- After 20 weeks of pregnancy

**Severe Pre-eclampsia (any one of these):**
- Blood pressure ≥160/110 mmHg
- Severe headache
- Visual disturbances (blurred vision, seeing spots)
- Upper abdominal pain (epigastric pain)
- Decreased urine output
- Proteinuria ≥3+

**Eclampsia:**
- Pre-eclampsia PLUS convulsions (seizures)
- Medical emergency — can be fatal

**Community Health Worker Actions.**
**Mild Pre-eclampsia (BP 140/90-159/109, no severe features):**
1. Refer to health facility within 24 hours
2. Advise rest on left side
3. Monitor for danger signs
4. Schedule weekly BP monitoring

**Severe Pre-eclampsia — EMERGENCY:**
1. **REFER URGENTLY** to hospital — this can progress to eclampsia rapidly
2. Keep woman on her left side
3. If trained and magnesium sulfate available:
   - Loading dose: 4g IV (slow, over 20 minutes) + 5g IM in each buttock (10g total)
   - If IV not possible: 5g IM in each buttock only
4. Do NOT leave the woman alone
5. Monitor breathing (>16 breaths/min) and knee reflex before each dose

**Eclampsia — LIFE-THREATENING EMERGENCY:**
1. **CALL FOR HELP IMMEDIATELY**
2. Protect from injury — do NOT restrain, do NOT put anything in mouth
3. Place on left side after convulsion
4. Maintain airway — extend neck, clear secretions
5. If magnesium sulfate available: give loading dose (as above)
6. **REFER URGENTLY** — woman needs delivery within hours

**Danger Signs — Refer Immediately.**
- Severe headache that won't go away
- Blurred vision or seeing spots
- Severe upper abdominal pain
- Sudden swelling of face or hands
- Convulsions (seizures)
- Loss of consciousness
- Vaginal bleeding
- Decreased or no fetal movement

**Antihypertensive Treatment (at facility).**
- Methyldopa 250-500 mg oral, 2-3 times daily (first-line)
- Nifedipine 10 mg oral (if BP ≥160/110 — for acute management)
- Hydralazine 5 mg IV (if available, for hypertensive crisis)

**Prevention.**
- Calcium supplementation: 1.5-2g/day in areas with low calcium intake
- Low-dose aspirin 75mg/day from 12 weeks if high risk (PREVENTIVE dose ONLY — analgesic or cardiac doses ≥300 mg are CONTRAINDICATED in pregnancy, especially third trimester; see pregnancy medication safety protocol)
- Regular antenatal visits with BP monitoring

**After Delivery.**
- Continue monitoring BP for at least 72 hours
- Risk of eclampsia continues up to 48 hours postpartum
- If BP remains high: continue antihypertensive, refer for follow-up

Source: WHO Recommendations for Prevention and Treatment of Pre-eclampsia and Eclampsia (2011, updated 2023)
