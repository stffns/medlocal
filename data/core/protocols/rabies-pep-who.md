# WHO: Rabies Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) for Community Health Workers

## Key Facts
- Rabies is **almost 100% fatal** once symptoms appear — PEP is life-saving
- **Wound washing is the single most effective first aid** — reduces rabies risk by up to 90%
- PEP must begin as soon as possible — there is NO upper time limit if exposure occurred
- Dogs cause 99% of human rabies deaths worldwide
- Children under 15 are the most frequent victims

## Exposure Categories (WHO Classification)

### Category I — No Exposure (no PEP needed)
- Touching or feeding an animal
- Licks on intact skin
- Contact with animal without direct skin break
- **Action:** No treatment needed, reassure patient

### Category II — Minor Exposure (PEP: vaccine only)
- Nibbling or minor scratches without bleeding
- Superficial bite (single, on arm or leg, not through clothing to skin)
- Licks on broken skin (cuts, grazes)
- **Action:** Wound washing + rabies vaccine series

### Category III — Severe Exposure (PEP: vaccine + immunoglobulin)
- Single or multiple bites that break the skin (transdermal)
- Deep scratches with bleeding
- Licks on mucous membranes (mouth, eyes, genitals)
- Bite or scratch on head, face, neck, hands, or genitals (high-risk sites)
- Bat contact (any scratch, bite, or mucous membrane exposure)
- Exposure to a confirmed rabid animal
- **Action:** Wound washing + rabies vaccine series + rabies immunoglobulin (RIG)

## Immediate First Aid — CRITICAL (do this BEFORE anything else)

### Wound Washing Protocol
1. **Wash the wound immediately** with soap and running water for **15 minutes**
   - This is the most effective single intervention — reduces viral load dramatically
   - Use any soap available (bar soap, liquid soap, detergent)
   - Flush thoroughly under running water (tap, bottle, clean stream)
2. **Apply antiseptic** after washing: povidone-iodine (Betadine), or 70% alcohol, or aqueous iodine
3. **Do NOT suture** bite wounds — leave open to drain
4. **Do NOT apply** traditional remedies, herbs, chili, ash, or irritants
5. **Do NOT cover tightly** — use loose, clean dressing

## Rabies Vaccine Schedule

### Intradermal (ID) — WHO Preferred (uses less vaccine, same efficacy)
- **2-site ID regimen:** 0.1 mL at 2 sites (left and right deltoid) on days 0, 3, and 7
- Total: 3 visits, 6 doses of 0.1 mL = less than 1 full vial
- Most cost-effective option for health posts

### Intramuscular (IM) — Alternative
- **4-dose Essen regimen:** 1 full vial IM (deltoid) on days 0, 3, 7, and 14
- For children: inject in anterolateral thigh (NOT gluteal — poor absorption)
- **NEVER inject in the gluteal area**

### Previously Vaccinated (completed PEP or pre-exposure series)
- 2 doses only: days 0 and 3 (ID or IM)
- **NO immunoglobulin needed** regardless of exposure category

## Rabies Immunoglobulin (RIG) — Category III Only

### When to Give
- **Only for Category III exposures** in unvaccinated persons
- Infiltrate as much as possible **directly into and around the wound**
- Give remaining dose IM at a site distant from vaccine
- Give on **day 0** (same day as first vaccine dose)
- If not available on day 0: give up to **day 7** after first vaccine dose (not after)

### Dosing
- **Human RIG (HRIG):** 20 IU/kg body weight
- **Equine RIG (ERIG):** 40 IU/kg body weight (more widely available, lower cost)
- If wound volume is small, dilute RIG with sterile saline to infiltrate fully

### If RIG is Unavailable
- **Do NOT delay vaccination** — start vaccine immediately
- Refer to facility with RIG as soon as possible (within 7 days of first vaccine)

## Animal Assessment

### High Risk — Treat as Rabid Until Proven Otherwise
- Unprovoked attack
- Dog/cat is stray, unknown, or unvaccinated
- Animal is behaving abnormally (aggression, drooling, paralysis, no fear of humans)
- Animal dies or disappears within 10 days
- Bat exposure (any contact)

### Lower Risk (still begin PEP, can stop if animal confirmed healthy)
- Provoked bite (stepped on animal, took food away)
- Dog/cat is healthy AND vaccinated AND can be observed for 10 days
- If animal remains healthy after 10 days AND was vaccinated: PEP can be discontinued

### Animals That Always Require Full PEP
- Bats (any species, any contact)
- Wild carnivores (jackals, foxes, raccoons, mongooses)
- Unobservable animals (stray, killed, escaped)

## Special Populations

### Children
- Same vaccine schedule and RIG dosing (weight-based) as adults
- Children are highest-risk group — always treat promptly
- Use anterolateral thigh for IM injection if under 2 years

### Pregnant/Breastfeeding Women
- Rabies PEP is safe and indicated in pregnancy and breastfeeding
- Rabies is fatal — **never withhold PEP due to pregnancy**

### Immunocompromised Patients (HIV, malnutrition)
- Give full PEP including RIG regardless of immune status
- Consider 5-dose IM schedule: days 0, 3, 7, 14, and 28
- Check antibody levels at day 14 if possible — may need additional doses
- **REFER** for follow-up

## Danger Signs — Refer Immediately
- Bite on face, head, neck, hands, or genitals (Category III — needs RIG)
- Multiple bites or deep wounds
- Bat exposure (any type)
- Wound showing signs of infection (spreading redness, pus, fever)
- Symptoms of rabies: hydrophobia (fear of water), aerophobia (fear of air/drafts), agitation, confusion, paralysis — **if symptoms present, rabies is almost certainly fatal**

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
- Do NOT wait to observe the animal before starting PEP — begin immediately
- Do NOT suture bite wounds
- Do NOT inject vaccine in gluteal area
- Do NOT withhold PEP because of pregnancy
- Do NOT skip RIG for Category III exposures
- Do NOT use RIG after day 7 post-first-vaccine

Source: WHO Position Paper on Rabies Vaccines and Immunoglobulins (2018); WHO Expert Consultation on Rabies, Third Report (2018); WHO Guide for Rabies Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (2024 update); MSF Clinical Guide Chapter 7: Bites and Stings
