# WHO: Rabies Prevention and Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP)

**CRITICAL — Rabies Is Nearly 100% Fatal Once Symptoms Appear.**
Prevention after exposure is the ONLY chance of survival.

**Animals That Carry Rabies.**
- Dogs (cause >99% of human rabies deaths)
- Cats, bats, foxes, jackals, mongooses, raccoons
- Any mammal can carry rabies

**Suspect Rabies in an Animal If.**
- Unprovoked attack or unusual aggression
- Excessive drooling, difficulty swallowing
- Staggering, paralysis
- Change in behavior (wild animal becomes tame, or tame animal becomes aggressive)
- Animal dies within 10 days of biting

**Exposure Categories and Treatment.**
**Category I — No risk:**
- Touching or feeding animals
- Licks on intact skin
- **No treatment needed**

**Category II — Moderate risk:**
- Minor scratches or abrasions without bleeding
- Nibbling on uncovered skin
- Licks on broken skin
- **Treatment:** Wound washing + rabies vaccine (4 doses)

**Category III — Severe risk (most bites):**
- Bites or scratches that break skin and bleed
- Licks on mucous membranes (mouth, eyes)
- ANY bat contact (bites can be undetectable)
- Bite on head, neck, hands, or genitals
- **Treatment:** Wound washing + rabies vaccine + Rabies Immunoglobulin (RIG)

**Immediate First Aid — Most Important Step.**
1. **WASH THE WOUND** with soap and running water for 15 minutes — this alone significantly reduces risk
2. Apply povidone-iodine 10% or alcohol 70% after washing
3. Do NOT suture bite wounds (leave open)
4. **REFER for PEP** as soon as possible — start within 24 hours ideally

**Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (at health facility).**
**Rabies Vaccine Schedule:**
- IM (deltoid): Days 0, 3, 7, 14 (4-dose Essen regimen)
- OR ID (intradermal): 2 sites on days 0, 3, 7 (WHO updated protocol — uses less vaccine)
- Never inject in buttock (poor absorption)

**Rabies Immunoglobulin (RIG) — Category III only:**
- Infiltrate as much as possible into and around the wound
- Remainder: IM at a site distant from vaccine
- Give on Day 0 (same day as first vaccine dose)
- If RIG not available immediately: can give up to Day 7

**If Animal Can Be Observed.**
- If dog/cat and can be observed for 10 days:
  - Start PEP immediately
  - If animal healthy after 10 days: can stop PEP (consult physician)
- If animal dies, disappears, or is wild: complete full PEP course

**Pre-Exposure Vaccination (for high-risk groups).**
- Veterinarians, animal handlers, lab workers, people in endemic areas
- 2 doses on days 0 and 7 (IM or ID)
- If exposed after pre-vaccination: only 2 booster doses needed, NO RIG

**Prevention.**
- Vaccinate dogs — most effective way to prevent human rabies
- Do not approach or touch stray animals
- Supervise children around animals
- Report animal bites to health authorities

Source: WHO Guide for Rabies Pre and Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (2018, updated 2024); WHO Expert Consultation on Rabies (2018)
