# Scabies — Intense Itching Worse at Night

**When to Suspect.**
- Intense itching, especially at NIGHT
- Small red bumps or burrows (thin lines) in skin
- Common locations: between fingers, wrists, elbows, armpits, waist, buttocks, genitals
- Multiple family members affected (highly contagious)
- Common in crowded living conditions

**Diagnosis.**
- Clinical: typical distribution + intense nocturnal itching = scabies
- If one family member has it, check ALL household members

**Treatment — Treat the WHOLE HOUSEHOLD Simultaneously.**
**First-Line: Permethrin Cream 5%:**
1. Bath first, dry skin
2. Apply cream from neck to toes (include between fingers, under nails, genitals)
3. In infants: also apply to scalp and face (avoid eyes and mouth)
4. Leave on for 8-14 hours (overnight), then wash off
5. Repeat application in 7 days (kills newly hatched mites)
6. Treat ALL household members at the same time, even those without symptoms

**Alternative: Benzyl Benzoate 25% Lotion:**
- Apply from neck down after bath
- Leave on 24 hours, wash off
- Repeat in 7 days
- For children: dilute to 12.5% (mix equal parts with water)

**For Itching (continues 2-4 weeks after treatment — this is normal):**
- Calamine lotion
- Oral cetirizine 10mg daily (adults) or 5mg (children 2-6 years)
- Itching after treatment does NOT mean treatment failed

**Crusted (Norwegian) Scabies — SEVERE:**
- Thick crusted skin with thousands of mites
- Common in immunocompromised (HIV)
- Extremely contagious
- Oral ivermectin 200 mcg/kg single dose + topical permethrin
- **Refer** for management

**Prevention and Hygiene.**
- Wash all clothing, bedding, towels in hot water on treatment day
- Items that cannot be washed: seal in plastic bag for 72 hours (mites die)
- Treat sexual partners
- Check school/community contacts

**Complications.**
- Secondary bacterial infection (impetigo) from scratching — treat with oral antibiotics
- Post-scabetic eczema — moisturize, short course low-potency steroid if severe

Source: WHO Guidelines on Scabies; MSF Clinical Guidelines Chapter 4; WHO Essential Medicines List
