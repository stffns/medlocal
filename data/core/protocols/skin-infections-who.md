# WHO: Common Skin Infections for Community Health Workers

## Bacterial Skin Infections

### Impetigo (School Sores)
- Red sores that burst, ooze, and form honey-colored crusts
- Very contagious — common in children
- **Treatment:**
  - Wash with soap and water, remove crusts gently
  - Apply mupirocin ointment 2% three times daily for 5 days
  - If extensive: Oral amoxicillin-clavulanate for 7 days
  - Keep child home until sores are dry

### Cellulitis
- Red, hot, swollen, painful area of skin
- Spreading redness
- May have fever
- **Treatment:**
  - Oral amoxicillin-clavulanate 625 mg twice daily for 7 days
  - If severe (fever, rapidly spreading, near eye): **REFER** for IV antibiotics
  - Mark the edge of redness with pen to track spread

### Abscess (Boil)
- Painful, red, swollen lump filled with pus
- Warm to touch
- **Treatment:**
  - If small and not fluctuant: warm compresses, may resolve
  - If fluctuant (movable fluid): incision and drainage needed — **refer** if not trained
  - Antibiotics usually NOT needed if properly drained

## Fungal Infections

### Ringworm (Tinea Corporis)
- Round, red, scaly patches with clear center
- Itchy, gradually enlarging
- NOT caused by a worm — it is a fungus
- **Treatment:** Clotrimazole cream 1% twice daily for 2-4 weeks
- Continue 1 week after skin looks clear

### Athlete's Foot (Tinea Pedis)
- Itchy, peeling skin between toes
- **Treatment:** Clotrimazole cream 1%, keep feet dry, cotton socks, open shoes

### Oral Thrush (Candida)
- White patches inside mouth, painful
- Common in: babies, HIV patients, patients on antibiotics
- **Treatment:** Nystatin oral suspension 100,000 IU 4x daily for 7 days
- In adults with no obvious cause: **offer HIV test**

## Scabies
- Intense itching, worse at night
- Tiny burrows or red bumps, especially between fingers, wrists, waist, genitals
- Highly contagious — treat entire household
- **Treatment:**
  - Permethrin cream 5%: apply from neck down, leave 8-14 hours, wash off
  - Repeat in 1 week
  - Wash all clothing and bedding in hot water
  - Treat ALL household members simultaneously

## Danger Signs — Refer
- Rapidly spreading redness with fever (severe cellulitis/necrotizing fasciitis)
- Skin infection near the eye
- Abscess on face, neck, or near spine
- Infection not improving after 5 days of treatment
- Non-healing ulcer (consider diabetes, HIV, tropical ulcer)
- Suspected herpes zoster (shingles) in HIV patient — may indicate advanced disease

Source: WHO/IMCI; MSF Clinical Guidelines Chapter on Skin Diseases; WHO Essential Medicines for Dermatology
