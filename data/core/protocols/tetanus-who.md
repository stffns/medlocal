# WHO: Tetanus for Community Health Workers

**When to Suspect Tetanus.**
- Stiffness of jaw (lockjaw — cannot open mouth) — earliest sign
- Stiffness of neck and back muscles
- Difficulty swallowing
- Muscle spasms triggered by noise, light, or touch
- Arched back (opisthotonos) during severe spasm
- History of wound, burn, or umbilical infection 3-21 days before symptoms

**Neonatal Tetanus.**
- Baby was normal at birth, then after 3-14 days:
  - Unable to suckle (lockjaw)
  - Stiffness, spasms
  - Excessive crying → then unable to cry
- Usually from unclean cord cutting or cord care with contaminated substances
- **REFER URGENTLY** — high mortality

**Treatment — REFER URGENTLY.**
Tetanus requires hospital care. Pre-referral:
1. **Quiet, dark room** — minimize stimulation (triggers spasms)
2. **Diazepam** 0.1-0.3 mg/kg IV slowly (to control spasms)
   - If no IV: rectal diazepam 0.5 mg/kg
3. **Tetanus Immunoglobulin (TIG)** 3000-5000 IU IM if available
4. Clean the wound thoroughly
5. **Metronidazole** 500 mg IV/oral every 8 hours (kills the bacteria)
6. Do NOT attempt to force jaw open

**Prevention — Most Important Role for CHW.**
**Wound Care:**
- Clean ALL wounds with soap and water
- Remove dead tissue and foreign bodies
- Tetanus-prone wounds: deep/puncture, soil-contaminated, burns, crush injuries, animal bites

**Tetanus Vaccination:**
- **Childhood:** DPT/Pentavalent at 6, 10, 14 weeks (see immunization protocol)
- **Booster:** At school entry and every 10 years
- **Pregnant women:** Tetanus toxoid per schedule (5 doses = lifelong protection)
- **After injury:** Tetanus toxoid if >5 years since last dose. Add TIG if wound is dirty and vaccination incomplete.

**Cord Care for Prevention of Neonatal Tetanus:**
- Clean delivery: sterile or clean blade to cut cord
- Clean cord care: apply chlorhexidine 7.1%, keep stump clean and dry
- Do NOT apply cow dung, ash, or traditional substances to cord

**Danger Signs.**
ALL suspected tetanus is an emergency:
- Jaw stiffness (cannot open mouth)
- Muscle spasms
- Difficulty breathing during spasms
- Newborn unable to suckle after day 3

Source: WHO Tetanus Position Paper (2017); WHO/UNICEF Maternal and Neonatal Tetanus Elimination Guidelines
