# Skin-Related Neglected Tropical Diseases (NTDs) for Community Health Workers

**Leprosy (Hansen's Disease).**
**When to Suspect:**
- Pale or reddish skin patches with LOSS OF SENSATION (numbness)
- Patient cannot feel touch, pain, or temperature on the patch
- Thickened nerves (palpable at elbow, behind ear, wrist)
- Muscle weakness in hands or feet

**Key Test for CHW:**
- Touch the skin patch with a cotton wisp — if patient does not feel it → suspect leprosy
- Test both the patch and normal skin for comparison

**Action:**
- **REFER** for confirmation and treatment
- Leprosy is CURABLE with multi-drug therapy (MDT)
- MDT is FREE through WHO in all countries
- Treatment: 6 months (paucibacillary) or 12 months (multibacillary)
- NOT highly contagious — requires prolonged close contact
- Reduce stigma: leprosy is curable, patient is non-infectious after first dose

**Buruli Ulcer.**
**When to Suspect:**
- Painless nodule or swelling, usually on limbs
- Progresses to painless ulcer with undermined edges
- Ulcer grows larger without treatment
- Common near rivers and wetlands in tropical Africa

**Action:**
- **REFER** early — small lesions can be cured with antibiotics alone
- Treatment: Rifampicin + Clarithromycin for 8 weeks
- Late cases may need surgery
- Early detection saves limbs

**Cutaneous Leishmaniasis.**
**When to Suspect:**
- Painless skin ulcer that does not heal (weeks to months)
- Started as small papule, grew into ulcer with raised edges
- History of sandfly bites in endemic area
- Common in: Middle East, North Africa, South America, Central Asia

**Action:**
- **REFER** for diagnosis (skin smear or biopsy)
- Treatment depends on species and severity
- Clean and dress the wound — prevent secondary infection

**Mycetoma (Madura Foot).**
**When to Suspect:**
- Painless swelling of the foot (or hand), slowly growing over months/years
- Draining sinuses (small holes) with discharge containing grains (black, white, or red)
- Common in tropical dry areas (Sahel, India, Mexico)

**Action:**
- **REFER** — requires long-term treatment
- Fungal mycetoma: antifungals for months + possible surgery
- Bacterial mycetoma (actinomycetoma): antibiotics for months
- Early referral prevents amputation

**Yaws.**
**When to Suspect:**
- Painless papilloma (raised, rough, raspberry-like lesion)
- Usually on legs, arms, face in children
- Endemic in tropical humid areas (Pacific, Southeast Asia, Africa)
- Highly contagious through direct skin contact

**Action:**
- Single dose oral azithromycin 30 mg/kg (max 2g) cures it
- Can be treated at community level
- **Report** — yaws is targeted for eradication by WHO

**General Rules for Skin NTDs.**
1. Any chronic wound or ulcer that does not heal in 2 weeks → suspect NTD
2. Any skin patch with numbness → suspect leprosy
3. Any painless growing nodule in tropical area → refer for assessment
4. REDUCE STIGMA — all skin NTDs are treatable
5. REPORT to health authorities — these are notifiable diseases

Source: WHO Training Guide for Skin NTDs (2018); WHO Strategic Framework for Skin NTDs; MSF Clinical Guidelines Chapter 4
