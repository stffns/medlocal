# WHO: Tuberculosis (TB) Management for Community Health Workers

**When to Suspect TB.**
A person with ANY of these symptoms for ≥2 weeks:
- Cough (with or without sputum)
- Fever (especially afternoon/evening)
- Night sweats
- Unexplained weight loss
- Fatigue

In HIV-positive patients: ANY cough, fever, weight loss, or night sweats — screen immediately.

In children: persistent cough >2 weeks, failure to thrive, fever, contact with TB patient.

**Diagnosis.**
- **Sputum test:** Collect 2 sputum samples (spot + early morning)
- GeneXpert/Xpert MTB/RIF (rapid molecular test) — result in 2 hours
- If sputum not possible: refer for chest X-ray
- Children: often sputum-negative — diagnose by symptoms + contact history + X-ray

**Treatment.**
**Drug-Susceptible TB (First-Line):**
**Standard 6-Month Regimen (2HRZE/4HR):**
**Intensive phase (first 2 months):**
- Isoniazid (H) + Rifampicin (R) + Pyrazinamide (Z) + Ethambutol (E)
- Daily, directly observed therapy (DOT)

**Continuation phase (next 4 months):**
- Isoniazid (H) + Rifampicin (R)
- Daily

**Dosing (Fixed-Dose Combinations by Weight):**
- 25-39 kg: 2 tablets daily
- 40-54 kg: 3 tablets daily
- 55-70 kg: 4 tablets daily
- >70 kg: 5 tablets daily

**TB in Children:**
- Same drugs, weight-based dosing
- Child-friendly formulations (dispersible tablets) available
- Duration: 4 months for non-severe TB (new WHO recommendation)

**Directly Observed Therapy (DOT).**
- A health worker or trained community member watches the patient swallow every dose
- Critical during intensive phase
- If patient misses doses: trace immediately, counsel, restart if needed

**TB Treatment Monitoring.**
- Sputum at month 2: if still positive → extend intensive phase or suspect drug resistance
- Sputum at month 5: if positive → treatment failure → refer for drug-susceptibility testing
- Weight monitoring monthly — weight gain = good response

**Drug-Resistant TB.**
- Suspect if: treatment failure, contact with drug-resistant case, relapse after treatment
- **REFER** — do not treat drug-resistant TB at community level
- GeneXpert detects rifampicin resistance

**TB Preventive Treatment (TPT).**
For contacts of TB patients who are NOT sick:
- **Preferred:** 3 months of Isoniazid + Rifapentine weekly (3HP)
- Alternative: 6 months of Isoniazid daily (6H)
- Priority: children <5 years, HIV-positive contacts, household contacts

**Side Effects — What to Watch For.**
- **Isoniazid:** tingling/numbness in hands/feet (peripheral neuropathy) → give pyridoxine (vitamin B6)
- **Rifampicin:** orange-red urine (normal, warn patient), nausea
- **Pyrazinamide:** joint pain
- **Ethambutol:** visual changes (blurred vision, color blindness) → stop and refer

**Danger Signs — Refer Immediately.**
- Coughing blood (hemoptysis)
- Severe difficulty breathing
- Jaundice (yellow eyes) — possible drug-induced hepatitis → STOP all TB drugs and refer
- Severe skin rash
- Visual changes while on Ethambutol
- Confusion or altered consciousness

**Infection Control.**
- Cough hygiene: cover mouth, cough into elbow
- Ventilation: keep windows and doors open
- Separate sleeping area for first 2 weeks of treatment
- TB patients become non-infectious after ~2 weeks of effective treatment
- Use N95/FFP2 mask when collecting sputum

Source: WHO Consolidated Guidelines on Tuberculosis Module 4: Treatment and Care (2022, updated 2024)
