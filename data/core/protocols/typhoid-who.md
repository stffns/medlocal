# WHO: Typhoid Fever for Community Health Workers

**When to Suspect Typhoid.**
- Sustained high fever (≥39°C) for >3 days, rising stepwise
- Headache
- Abdominal pain (may be generalized or right lower quadrant)
- Constipation (early) OR diarrhea (later — "pea soup" stools)
- Loss of appetite, malaise
- Rose-colored spots on chest/abdomen (rare but characteristic)
- Relative bradycardia (pulse slower than expected for fever level)

**Diagnosis.**
- Blood culture (gold standard) — if available
- Widal test: unreliable, high false-positive rate — use with caution
- Do malaria RDT to rule out malaria (can coexist)

**Treatment.**
**Uncomplicated Typhoid:**
**First-line:**
- **Azithromycin** 500 mg once daily for 7 days (adults)
- Children: 10-20 mg/kg/day for 7 days
**Alternative:**
- **Ciprofloxacin** 500 mg twice daily for 7 days (adults) — avoid in children if possible
- **Ceftriaxone** 2g IV/IM daily for 10-14 days (if oral not tolerated)

**Supportive Care:**
- Adequate fluids — ORS, water, soups
- Paracetamol for fever (NOT aspirin — bleeding risk)
- Soft diet as tolerated
- Rest

**Complications — REFER URGENTLY:**
- **Intestinal perforation:** Sudden severe abdominal pain, rigid abdomen, shock — surgical emergency
- **Intestinal hemorrhage:** Blood in stool, signs of shock
- **Encephalopathy:** Confusion, delirium

**Follow-Up.**
- Fever should improve within 3-5 days of treatment
- If fever persists >5 days on treatment: suspect drug resistance — **REFER**
- Relapse occurs in 5-10% — re-treat with same regimen

**Prevention.**
- Safe drinking water (boil or treat)
- Proper sanitation — use latrines
- Handwashing with soap before eating and after toilet
- Proper food handling — cook thoroughly, eat hot
- Typhoid conjugate vaccine (TCV) available for endemic areas

**Key Differences from Malaria.**
| Feature | Typhoid | Malaria |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Fever pattern | Sustained, stepwise rise | Cyclical, with chills |
| Abdominal symptoms | Common (pain, constipation) | Less common |
| Headache | Prominent | Present but less severe |
| Rash | Rose spots (rare) | No rash |
| Diagnosis | Blood culture | RDT or microscopy |

Source: WHO Guidelines for the Management of Typhoid Fever (2003, updated 2019); WHO Typhoid Vaccine Position Paper (2018)
