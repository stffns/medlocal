# WHO: Urinary Tract Infections for Community Health Workers

## When to Suspect UTI
- Pain or burning when urinating (dysuria)
- Frequent urination, small amounts
- Urgency (sudden need to urinate)
- Cloudy, dark, or bloody urine
- Lower abdominal pain or discomfort
- Bad-smelling urine

## Classification

### Simple UTI (lower — bladder infection/cystitis)
- Dysuria, frequency, urgency
- No fever
- No back pain
- Most common in women

### Complicated UTI / Pyelonephritis (upper — kidney infection)
- Fever ≥38°C
- Flank/back pain (costovertebral angle tenderness)
- Nausea, vomiting
- May follow untreated simple UTI
- **REFER** for IV antibiotics

## Treatment

### Simple UTI in Women
- **Nitrofurantoin** 100 mg twice daily for 5 days (first-line)
- OR **Amoxicillin-clavulanate** 625 mg twice daily for 5 days
- Increase fluid intake
- Paracetamol for pain

### UTI in Men
- Less common — may indicate underlying problem
- Treat for 7 days (same antibiotics)
- **REFER** for further investigation (prostate, kidney stones)

### UTI in Pregnancy
- Screen at first antenatal visit (urine dipstick)
- Treat promptly — untreated UTI in pregnancy can cause preterm labor
- **Amoxicillin-clavulanate** 625 mg twice daily for 7 days
- OR **Nitrofurantoin** 100 mg twice daily for 7 days (NOT near term — avoid in last 4 weeks)
- Follow-up urine test after treatment

### UTI in Children
- Fever with no obvious source + irritability in infant = consider UTI
- Older child: dysuria, wetting after being dry, abdominal pain
- **REFER** for urine culture and investigation (UTI in children may indicate structural abnormality)

## Prevention
- Drink plenty of water (6-8 glasses daily)
- Urinate frequently — do not hold urine
- Wipe front to back (women)
- Urinate after sexual intercourse
- Avoid douching or vaginal deodorants
- Cotton underwear, avoid tight clothing

## Danger Signs — Refer
- Fever with back pain (pyelonephritis)
- Blood in urine with fever
- Vomiting — cannot take oral medication
- UTI not improving after 3 days of treatment
- Recurrent UTIs (3+ per year)
- UTI in children, men, or pregnant women

Source: WHO IMAI Acute Care Guidelines; WHO Essential Medicines; MSF Clinical Guidelines
