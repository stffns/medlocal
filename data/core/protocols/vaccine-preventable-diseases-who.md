# WHO: Vaccine-Preventable Diseases — Recognition for Community Health Workers

**How to Recognize — Quick Reference.**
**Measles:**
- Fever + generalized rash + cough/runny nose/red eyes
- Rash starts on face, spreads down
- Ask: vaccination status? Contact with case?
- See dedicated measles protocol

**Diphtheria:**
- Sore throat with grey-white membrane on tonsils
- "Bull neck" swelling
- Difficulty breathing, drooling
- **REFER URGENTLY** — can obstruct airway
- Give antitoxin + penicillin at facility

**Pertussis (Whooping Cough):**
- Cough lasting >2 weeks with paroxysms (fits of rapid coughing)
- Characteristic "whoop" sound when breathing in after coughing fit
- Vomiting after coughing
- Dangerous in infants <6 months
- Treatment: Azithromycin 10 mg/kg/day for 5 days
- **REFER** infants under 6 months — risk of apnea

**Neonatal Tetanus:**
- Newborn normal at birth, then unable to suckle (day 3-14)
- Stiffness, spasms
- See tetanus protocol — **REFER URGENTLY**

**Polio:**
- Sudden onset of floppy paralysis (limb goes limp)
- Usually one leg, asymmetric
- Fever before paralysis
- **REFER URGENTLY** + report immediately (polio is targeted for eradication)

**Yellow Fever:**
- Fever, headache, muscle pain, nausea
- May improve briefly then: jaundice, bleeding, dark vomit
- Endemic in tropical Africa and South America
- **REFER** if jaundice or bleeding
- Prevention: single-dose vaccine (lifetime protection)

**Hepatitis B (in newborns):**
- Prevented by birth dose vaccine within 24 hours
- See hepatitis protocol

**For ALL Suspected Vaccine-Preventable Diseases.**
1. **REPORT** to health authority immediately — these are notifiable diseases
2. Check vaccination records of the patient AND contacts
3. Vaccinate unvaccinated contacts (ring vaccination)
4. Isolate the patient when appropriate
5. Treat complications per specific protocols

**Key Message for Community.**
- Vaccines PREVENT these diseases — they do not cause them
- Mild fever after vaccination is NORMAL and means the vaccine is working
- A vaccinated child who gets the disease gets a MILDER form with fewer complications
- Complete the full schedule — partial vaccination gives incomplete protection

Source: WHO Immunization in Practice; WHO Disease-Specific Position Papers; WHO IMCI Guidelines
