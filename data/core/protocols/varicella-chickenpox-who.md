# WHO: Varicella (Chickenpox) — Recognition and Management

Also known as: varicela, catapora, chicken pox, herpes zoster virus primary infection.

**How to Recognise Chickenpox (Varicella).**
- **Vesicular rash**: small fluid-filled blisters ("granitos con agua", "ampollitas") on a red base, starting on the trunk/face, spreading to limbs
- **Crops in different stages**: new vesicles appear over 3-5 days alongside older crusted lesions — this is the hallmark that distinguishes varicella from other rashes
- **Intensely itchy** (pruriginoso)
- Mild fever (37.5-39°C), malaise, headache — usually 1-2 days before rash
- Rash evolves: red macule → papule → vesicle (clear fluid) → pustule (cloudy) → crust (5-7 days)
- Lesions are superficial, NOT deep; fluid is clear initially

**Differential Diagnosis — Do NOT Confuse With:**
- **Measles (sarampión)**: flat maculopapular rash (NO vesicles/blisters), Koplik spots in mouth, higher fever, cough/coryza/conjunctivitis. Measles rash does NOT have fluid-filled blisters.
- **Impetigo**: honey-colored crusts, localized, no vesicle stage
- **Scabies**: burrows, interdigital, nocturnal itch, no vesicles on trunk
- **Insect bites**: papular, grouped, exposed areas only
- **Hand-foot-mouth**: vesicles on palms/soles/mouth only (enterovirus)

**Treatment — CHW Level (uncomplicated, healthy child).**
1. **Symptomatic care only** — chickenpox in healthy children is self-limiting (5-10 days)
2. **Fever**: Paracetamol 10-15 mg/kg every 6 hours. Do NOT give aspirin (Reye syndrome risk) or ibuprofen (increased risk of necrotizing fasciitis in varicella — controversial but avoid)
3. **Itch relief**: calamine lotion, cool baths, keep nails short to prevent scratching and secondary infection
4. **Hydration**: encourage fluids, soft foods if mouth lesions present
5. **Isolation**: infectious from 2 days before rash until all lesions crusted (usually 5-7 days). Keep away from pregnant women, newborns, and immunocompromised persons
6. **Acyclovir is NOT routinely recommended** for healthy children with uncomplicated varicella (Cochrane review: modest benefit, many unanswered questions)

**REFER — When Chickenpox Needs Hospital Care.**
- Neonate (under 1 month) with varicella or born to mother with varicella within 5 days before or 2 days after delivery — EMERGENCY
- Immunocompromised child (HIV, chemotherapy, steroids) — acyclovir IV needed
- Secondary bacterial skin infection (spreading redness, pus, fever rising after day 4)
- Pneumonia symptoms (cough, tachypnea, chest indrawing) — varicella pneumonia
- Neurological signs (ataxia, encephalitis, severe headache, confusion)
- Severe dehydration from oral lesions preventing fluid intake
- Pregnant woman exposed to varicella — needs specialist assessment for VZIG within 96 hours

**Source:** Varicella-Zoster Virus StatPearls (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK448191/); Cochrane Review: Acyclovir for varicella in healthy children (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8407192/); WHO Pocket Book of Hospital Care for Children (2013, 2nd ed.).
