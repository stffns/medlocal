# WHO: Wound Care and First Aid for Community Health Workers

**Wound Assessment.**
**Classification:**
- **Clean wound:** Knife cut, surgical wound — low infection risk
- **Contaminated wound:** Fall, scrape, animal bite — moderate risk
- **Dirty wound:** Soil contamination, old wound, puncture — high risk
- **Infected wound:** Pus, redness, warmth, swelling, pain — needs treatment

**Basic Wound Care (for clean/contaminated wounds).**
**Steps:**
1. **Wash your hands** with soap and water. Wear gloves if available
2. **Stop bleeding:** Apply direct pressure with clean cloth for 10-15 minutes
3. **Clean the wound:** Wash with clean running water for 5 minutes
   - Remove dirt and debris gently
   - Use soap around (not inside) the wound
   - Do NOT use alcohol, iodine, or hydrogen peroxide inside wound (damages tissue)
4. **Dry** surrounding skin
5. **Cover** with clean, dry dressing
6. **Change dressing** daily or when wet/dirty

**Do NOT:**
- Do NOT close dirty wounds with stitches or tape (traps infection)
- Do NOT apply traditional remedies, cow dung, ash, or herbs
- Do NOT remove embedded objects (refer)

**Tetanus Prevention.**
- Check tetanus vaccination status
- If wound is dirty/deep AND vaccination incomplete or unknown:
  - Give Tetanus Toxoid injection
  - If high risk: also give Tetanus Immunoglobulin (TIG) if available

**Signs of Wound Infection — Treat or Refer.**
- Increasing pain after day 2
- Spreading redness around wound
- Pus or cloudy discharge
- Bad smell
- Fever
- Red streaks from wound toward body (lymphangitis — URGENT)

**Treatment of Infected Wound:**
1. Open wound if closed (remove stitches/tape)
2. Clean with saline or clean water
3. Apply wet dressing (saline-soaked gauze) — change twice daily
4. Oral antibiotic: amoxicillin-clavulanate 625 mg twice daily for 5-7 days
5. Pain: paracetamol
6. Tetanus prophylaxis if not up to date

**Animal Bites.**
**Dog Bites (rabies risk):**
1. **Wash immediately** with soap and running water for 15 minutes — this is the most important step
2. Apply povidone-iodine or alcohol after washing
3. Do NOT suture bite wounds (leave open)
4. **REFER for rabies post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP):**
   - Category II (superficial bite): Rabies vaccine only
   - Category III (deep bite, multiple bites, bat contact): Rabies vaccine + Rabies Immunoglobulin
5. Start antibiotics: amoxicillin-clavulanate
6. Tetanus prophylaxis

**Cat Bites:**
- High infection risk (deep puncture wounds)
- Wash thoroughly, do NOT suture
- Start antibiotics: amoxicillin-clavulanate
- Watch closely for infection (develops fast)

**Human Bites:**
- Very high infection risk
- Wash thoroughly
- Start antibiotics: amoxicillin-clavulanate
- Check for hepatitis B and HIV exposure risk
- Refer if deep or on hand/face

**Abscess.**
1. If fluctuant (soft, movable fluid): needs incision and drainage
2. If not trained: **refer** for drainage
3. After drainage: pack with saline-soaked gauze, change daily
4. Oral antibiotics may NOT be needed if drained adequately

**Bleeding Control.**
**External Bleeding:**
1. Apply direct pressure with clean cloth
2. Elevate the limb
3. If bleeding through, add more cloth on top (do NOT remove first cloth)
4. If severe bleeding from limb and pressure fails: apply tourniquet above the wound
   - Note the time — loosen every 30 minutes
   - **REFER URGENTLY**

**Nosebleed (Epistaxis):**
1. Sit upright, lean slightly forward
2. Pinch soft part of nose firmly for 10-15 minutes
3. Breathe through mouth
4. Do NOT tilt head back (blood goes to stomach)
5. If not stopping after 20 minutes: **refer**

**Danger Signs — Refer Immediately.**
- Deep wound exposing bone, tendon, or joint
- Wound with embedded object (glass, metal, wood)
- Wound won't stop bleeding after 15 minutes of pressure
- Animal bite to face, neck, hand, or genitals
- Red streaks spreading from wound
- Bite from potentially rabid animal
- Wound with loss of sensation or movement beyond it

Source: WHO Basic Emergency Care (2018); WHO Surgical Care at District Hospital (2003); WHO Guidelines on Rabies Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (2018)
